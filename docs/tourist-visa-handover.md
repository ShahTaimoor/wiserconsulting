# Tourist Visa form: handover

For the engineer who will review and deploy this. It describes what is built, what is not, how to switch it on, what it needs on production, what it changed in existing files, and what still needs a decision.

Branch: `feature/tourist-visa-form`. Question specification: [tourist-visa-form-spec.md](tourist-visa-form-spec.md). Config version at the time of writing: `1.1.0` (the design was approved as final by the senior on 2026-11-01, section 10; `1.1.0` only added the currency helper flag, section 2.5). This describes the state after step 8f, the currency helper (section 2.5), the AI-assisted cover letter for administrators (step 8g, section 2.6) the AI-assisted itinerary (section 2.7) and the PDF foundation with the application summary PDF (step 8h-1, section 2.8): the applicant's whole journey is built (fill in the sections, upload the documents, review, submit; sections 2.1 and 2.2), and so is the admin side: a list, an Overview with the call details first, files by a 2-minute link, a status and an activity log (section 2.3), and the **correction of a submitted application's answers with a full audit trail, notes, a call log and flags** (section 2.4). The **application summary PDF** is built for both sides (section 2.8). The **cover letter PDF** and the **itinerary PDF** (steps 8h-2 and 8h-3, administrators only, designed with a letterhead and a timetable of coloured day blocks) are built too (section 2.8). The **entry points** (step 8i: the assessment popup, the footer link and the sign-up and login pages, section 2.9) are built too. The security review and everything after are still to build (section 14).

## 1. At a glance

- A signed-in user fills in a UK visitor (tourist) visa application, one section at a time, and can leave and come back. Answers are saved on the server per section ("Save and continue").
- **The design is final.** The senior approved the design as final on **2026-11-01**: every question, option list, follow-up and rule, the four Part 9 ticks, the final submit declaration and the text shown after submitting. The `draft` markers and notes were removed from the config (version `1.0.0`, from `0.12.0-draft`) **without changing a word, option, rule or id**, and **no `tbc` is left**, so the release check exits **0**. Version `1.0.1` then reworded the config's `limits.note` to state the real limits and changed nothing else. Version `1.1.0` then added the optional `currencyHelper` flag to the ten money questions (section 2.5), again without changing a word, option, rule or id. The choices that were drafts are kept in section 10 as the record of what was approved. A later change of wording comes from the senior as a question number or part; section 10.10 says how to make it.
- **The server's switch is the single source of truth** (section 4). The backend variable `TOURIST_VISA_ENABLED` is **off unless it is exactly `true`**; with it off the whole API is inert (every request gets one fixed "closed" answer and nothing is read, stored or logged) **except one public call, `GET /api/tourist-visa/status`, which answers `{ "open": false }`**. **The client asks that call and follows it**: while the server says closed, or the answer cannot be had, the Tourist Visa pages show "This application is not available yet", the admin menu has no Tourist Visa entry and the admin pages say the same, and no other Tourist Visa call is made. The client keeps its own config hold and its preview flag (section 4.2), but they can only close it further: a client build can never open a closed server. **Release and rollback are one switch on the backend (section 9).**
- Nothing links to the form yet. It can only be reached by typing `/tourist-visa`.
- **Administrators can have a cover letter written** (section 2.6), by an AI (Groq) from an allowlisted set of facts with no names or identifiers, or by a template when the AI is off or fails, then check, edit and approve it. It is admin only, off unless `AI_ENABLED` is `true`, and submitting an application never depends on it.
- **Administrators can also have a proposed day-by-day itinerary written** (section 2.7), in the same way: the server writes the headings (the days and their dates), an AI returns one short description for each from a smaller allowlist (the dates, the purpose, the places and plans described, the kind of stay), or a template does when the AI is off or fails. It is admin only, shares the AI allowance and switches with the cover letter, and the PDF step will use its approved version.
- **The application can be downloaded as a PDF** (section 2.8): an administrator from the Overview ("Export application (PDF)") and the applicant, once submitted, from their confirmation page ("Download a copy (PDF)"). It is made on demand in memory, never stored, rate limited and audited, and it follows the layout of an official printout without looking like one (our own logo and colours, a footer saying it is not an official document). The cover letter has a PDF too (step 8h-2, administrators only; a version that is not approved is printed with a DRAFT watermark); the itinerary has one too (step 8h-3: a designed timetable, the same draft rule). Both are for the team only.
- **Money questions have a currency helper** (section 2.5): "Enter in another currency" works out the pound amount from today's rate. It needs one new authenticated route, `GET /api/tourist-visa/rates`, which asks two outside exchange-rate providers for rates and sends them nothing about the applicant.
- It adds seven new database collections (`touristvisaapplications`, `touristvisaconfigsnapshots`, `touristvisaaudit`, `touristvisachanges`, `touristvisanotes`, `touristvisacoverletters`, `touristvisaitineraries`) and does not change the data of any existing feature. The only existing collection it reads is `users`.

## 2. What is built

**Questions: Parts 1 to 10 of the spec, in 23 sections** (10D, the documents, is a section of two file questions). Section 4A (spouse or partner) only applies when 2.13 is married, civil partnership or unmarried partner, so an applicant sees 22 or 23 sections, and the dashboard counts only the ones that apply (for example "5 of 22 sections saved").

| Part | Sections |
| --- | --- |
| 1 Travel information | 1 |
| 2 Personal information | 2A name and email, 2B identity, 2C address and living situation, 2D telephone |
| 3 Passport | 3 |
| 4 Family | 4A spouse or partner, 4B dependants, 4C parents |
| 5 Employment | 5 |
| 6 Money, family in the UK, accommodation | 6A income and expenditure, 6B family in the UK, 6C accommodation, 6D public funds |
| 7 Travel history (UK) | 7 |
| 8 Travel history (other countries) | 8 |
| 9 Other history (security and character) | 9A espionage, sabotage and war crimes, 9B terrorism and extremism, 9C other conduct and character |
| 10 Penalties and closing questions | 10A convictions and other penalties, 10B extra information, 10C language and National Insurance number, 10D documents (passport and funds files) |

211 questions and 16 repeating groups in all.

**One config drives both sides.** The questions live in `shared/tourist-visa/questions.json` and the rules in `shared/tourist-visa/engine.js`. The client form and the server validation both run the same engine, so they cannot disagree. Changing a question, an option list or a condition means editing the JSON, not code.

**Save and resume.** Each section is validated and saved on its own. The dashboard (`/tourist-visa`) shows every section as saved, not started or needs review, and "Continue" goes to the first section that is not done. A saved section is flagged "needs review" automatically when something it depends on changes (for example, moving the leave date in section 1 flags the accommodation dates in 6C).

**Gates (a Yes/No or a choice decides what appears).** When an answer changes and hides questions, the server removes the hidden answers from the database. Examples: 2.13 relationship status shows or hides the whole of section 4A; 5.1 employment status shows its own block of questions (employed: 5.2 to 5.9; self-employed, student, retired, and homemaker / not working / other each have theirs, section 10.3); 6.1 shows 6.2 for savings and an optional monthly other-income amount (6.1a) for other income; a Yes on 1.2, 1.3, 2.23, 2.30 or 2.31, or on the new 4.8 ("can you give your parents' details?"), opens its follow-up questions (section 10.2); a Yes on 6.5, 6.6 or 6.8, and a No on 6.7, open structured follow-ups (section 10.3); a Yes on 7.1, 7.3 or 7.6 opens a list of entries (visas, leave-to-remain applications, breaches), and 7.4, 7.7 and 8.2 open their questions and groups; a number above 0 at 8.1 shows the optional tick boxes of 8.1a (section 10.4); each of the ten Yes/No questions of Part 9 (9.1 to 9.10) opens a required details box on a Yes (section 10.5); in 10A **each ticked choice of 10.1 opens its own required details box** and unticking it removes the box and its answer, while the exclusive choice "None of these" cannot be ticked with the others; Other at 10.4 opens a required "which language" box and a Yes at 10.5 opens a required box for the National Insurance number (section 10.6). A section with no visible question is hidden everywhere (dashboard, progress count, Back and Next, direct address) and cannot be saved. **An answer that is hidden only because its gate has never been answered is kept, not removed**: when a new Yes/No is put in front of questions that already hold saved answers (as 4.8 was put in front of the parents), those answers wait, untouched, until the Yes/No is answered.

**Repeating groups.** Add another and Remove for companions (1.3, up to 10), other names (2.3, up to 10), other nationalities (2.23, up to 5), medical treatments (2.31, up to 5), dependants (4B), parents (4C, opened by 4.8, 1 to 4), payers (6.5, up to 5), relatives in the UK (6.6, up to 10), places of stay (6C), public funds received (6.8, up to 5), UK visas issued (7.2, up to 10), leave-to-remain applications (7.3, up to 5), breaches of UK immigration law (7.6, up to 5), immigration problems (7.7) and other-country trips (8.2). The UK visits (7.5a to 7.5c) are count-driven: the number typed at 7.5 (1 to 99) decides how many visit entries there are, with no Add or Remove, **but at most the 20 most recent visits are listed**: a number above 20 asks for exactly 20 entries, the question's help text and a note above the entries say so, and the number typed is stored as given.

**Cross-section and date rules.** Passport expiry (3.4) must be after the issue date and after the planned leave date (1.7). Places of stay must fall inside the travel dates (1.6 to 1.7) and must not overlap. Past-event dates cannot be in the future, and "in the past 10 years" dates cannot be older than 10 years.

**Encryption at rest.** 36 answers, and the original file name of every uploaded document, are encrypted: the national identity card number (2.19), the UK driving licence number (2.30a), the medical treatment text (2.31b), the passport number (3.1), and, new in step 7d, the UK visa reference number (7.2c), the leave-to-remain application reference number (7.3f) and the breach details (7.6d), and, new in step 8b-1, the ten Yes/No answers of Part 9 (9.1 to 9.10) and their ten details boxes (9.1a to 9.10a), and, new in step 8b-2, the penalties checklist (10.1) and its six details boxes (10.1a to 10.1f), the National Insurance Yes/No (10.5) and the number itself (10.5a). The original name of each uploaded file (section 2.1) is encrypted the same way. The four declaration ticks (9.11 to 9.14), the two extra-information boxes (10.2, 10.3) and the language (10.4, 10.4a) are not encrypted. A National Insurance number is stored in capitals with no spaces, whatever the applicant typed. The medical text and the three Part 7 answers sit inside group entries, and each entry is encrypted on its own. They are encrypted before they are stored (AES-256-GCM, stored as `enc:v1:...`). The ciphertext is bound to the application and the field, so it cannot be copied into another field or entry. Everything else is stored as typed. Answers are never written to logs, localStorage, Redux, URLs, the console or analytics.

**Interface.** Accessible labels, errors and focus handling, an error summary, light and dark mode, and a layout that works at 375 px. Leaving a section with unsaved changes opens a dialog in the site's own style ("Stay on this page" / "Leave without saving"); saving shows a "Section saved" toast. The feature uses no native browser alert, confirm or prompt.

**Declarations.** A tick the applicant must give (type `ACK`, the four of Part 9) records what was agreed: the SHA-256 hash of the exact words shown (the tick text and its guidance), the time, the user and the config version, on the application as `declarations.<question id>`. No IP address or browser data is kept, and the applicant is never sent the records. If the wording is later changed in the config, a tick given for the old words is shown unticked and its section shows "Needs review" until it is ticked again; the stored answer is not touched. The exact wording of the ticks and of the final submit declaration lives in one place, `shared/tourist-visa/questions.json` (section 10.5).

**Documents (steps 8c-1 and 8c-2).** The passport copy and the proof of funds are uploaded through the server to private storage, from the documents page (section 10D). Details in section 2.1.

**Preview markers.** In preview mode (section 4) a question or repeating group shows a **TBC** badge when it has a real unknown, and a **Draft options** badge when it carries a draft (an option list, follow-up questions or a rule we chose). **The approved config carries neither, so no badge appears today**; the markers still work and show again if a `tbc` or a `draft` is ever added to the config (section 10.10).

**Tests.** See section 8.

### 2.1 Document uploads (backend step 8c-1, page step 8c-2)

The backend was built in step 8c-1 and the page in step 8c-2. Nothing outside the application links to it yet. It sits behind the same switches as everything else (section 4): while `TOURIST_VISA_ENABLED` is not `true`, all three routes answer the fixed 404 `FEATURE_CLOSED`, before any file is read.

**Routes** (under `/api/tourist-visa`, signed-in user, own application only):

| Route | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `POST /applications/me/documents` | One file per request, `multipart/form-data` with two parts: `slot` (`passport` or `funds`) and `file`. Answers 201 with the new file's metadata. |
| `GET /applications/me/documents` | The applicant's files (metadata only) and the limits, so the screen can show them. |
| `DELETE /applications/me/documents/:docId` | Deletes one file from the draft and from storage. |

The applicant is only ever sent `id`, `slot`, `name`, `size`, `type` and `uploadedAt`. **No link, no URL and no storage id (public id) is ever returned**; `GET /applications/me` shows the same metadata in its `documents` list. The routes are `/applications/me/documents` (not `/application/documents`) to match the existing `/applications/me/...` routes.

**Limits** live in one file, `backend/config/touristVisaDocuments.js`; change a number there and everything follows.

| Limit | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Types | PDF, JPG, PNG, decided by the file's first bytes, never by its name or the type the browser declared. A text file renamed `.pdf` is refused; a real PNG named `.pdf` is accepted as a PNG. |
| Size of one file | **4 MB**. Empty files are refused. |
| Files | passport (10.6): up to 3; funds (10.7): up to 10. Total of all files of one application: 40 MB. |
| Temporary link | 2 minutes |
| Upload rate | 40 uploads per hour per signed-in user (in memory per server process, like the other limiters here) |

**Why 4 MB, and the Vercel note.** The backend may run on Vercel serverless, where a request body cannot be larger than 4.5 MB; a 4 MB file plus its multipart wrapper stays under that. **On a normal server (not serverless) the limit can be raised to 5 MB:** change `MAX_FILE_BYTES` in that one file. The route reads one file per request, in memory, with multer's limits set so an oversize file is stopped as it arrives (the declared length is checked first). Hosting is still to be confirmed before 8c-2; if the backend does not run serverless, a larger limit and direct-to-storage uploads can be reconsidered.

**Error codes** (sent as `code` next to the usual `message`; the messages are fixed text, so nothing the caller sent comes back): `WRONG_TYPE` (415), `FILE_TOO_LARGE` (413), `FILE_EMPTY`, `FILE_REQUIRED`, `INVALID_SLOT`, `INVALID_UPLOAD` (400), `TOO_MANY_FILES`, `TOTAL_TOO_LARGE`, `DOCUMENTS_LOCKED` (409), `DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND` (404), `TOO_MANY_UPLOADS` (429), `UPLOAD_FAILED` (502), `STORAGE_UNAVAILABLE` (503). `AppError` gained an optional `code` for this; responses of every other route are unchanged.

**Section 10D, the page (step 8c-2).** Section 10D "Documents" is the last section of Part 10 and has two `FILE` questions: **10.6 "Your passport or travel document"** (slot `passport`, up to 3 files; a clear scan or photo of the page with your photo and personal details) and **10.7 "Evidence of funds"** (slot `funds`, up to 10 files; documents showing money you can clearly access, for example recent bank statements). "Save and continue" on 10C goes to 10D; the dashboard lists it as the last row of Part 10. The page shows each slot with its guidance, the accepted types and limits (read from `GET .../documents`, never written in the client), the files uploaded so far (name, type, size, time), an Upload button per slot and a Remove button per file. One file goes up per request; the browser first checks type and size and says why in plain words, and the server (which looks at the real content) stays the authority: every code of section 2.1 has its own message. Removing a file asks first, in the site's own dialog. If the applicant tries to leave while a file is still uploading, the site's own "unsaved changes" dialog asks (the upload is cancelled if they leave). Results are announced in a live region and as toasts (fixed wording, never a file name), keyboard focus moves to the new file or back to the slot's Upload button, and the layout holds at 375 px in light and dark. No native alert, confirm or prompt is used, and the applicant never sees a link, a URL or a storage id.

**The file question type.** `FILE` was already in the engine's type list; 8c-2 gave it its rules. A `FILE` question needs a `slot` (lower-case letters, one question per slot) and may have no options, rules, condition, group or encryption. It stores **nothing** in the answers: its id is not an answer key (a request that sends one is refused like any unknown key), pruning ignores it, and `required` means "at least one file before the application can be submitted". The engine gained `getFileQuestions`, `isFilesOnlySection`, `documentsStatus` and `documentsComplete` (all in `shared/tourist-visa/engine.js`, with types).

**Status of 10D.** It is worked out from the files, never stored: **Not started** (no file), **In progress** (one slot has a file), **Saved** (both slots have at least one). The server computes it when it reads the application (a new `in_progress` status) and the dashboard computes the same from the file list, so they agree; "Saved" counts in "x of y sections saved". A draft can always be saved and left without files: `PUT /applications/me/sections/10D` is refused (400, "This section has no answers to save") because there is nothing to save. The resume point stays on 10D until another section needs attention, and 10D is the last section, so it is also where an applicant with everything saved resumes.

**For the submit step (8d).** The rule "both documents are required" is enforced at submit, not on save. The application the applicant reads has `documentsComplete` (true when every file question has at least one file), and the same rule is `engine.documentsComplete(config, documents)`. Step 8d must refuse to submit until it is true.

**Developer tool: `backend/scripts/tourist-visa-doc-link.js`.** Before the admin Overview existed (step 8e, section 2.3) this was the only way to open an uploaded file; it is still there for testing the private storage by hand, and the only way to do that without the admin pages. It is not a route and nothing calls it. `node scripts/tourist-visa-doc-link.js <applicationId> <documentId> --confirm` prints the two ids, the time the link expires and a signed link that works for 2 minutes (the ids are in the database, or the document id is the `id` that `GET .../documents` shows). It **refuses to run without `--confirm`** (or `TOURIST_VISA_DOC_LINK_CONFIRM=yes`), because the link opens a real applicant's file for anyone who holds it; it reads `backend/.env` only after that check, prints only the ids, the expiry and the link (the link carries the API key and a signature, never the secret, and the tool refuses to print a link that contains one), and shows only an error's code or type on failure, never its message. It writes nothing anywhere: it is not a record of who opened a file, which is why the admin route (section 2.3) writes an audit row for every link it makes. It is for developer machines; do not use it on a pasted command line.

**Locking and limits.** Uploads and deletes are allowed only while the application is a draft; a submitted (or later) application answers `DOCUMENTS_LOCKED`. The per-slot count, the total size and the draft status are checked **in the same database write that adds the file**, so two uploads at the same moment cannot both pass a check that only one should. The server also checks before it stores anything, so a refused file is never uploaded.

**No file is left behind.** If the database write does not happen (a limit hit by a parallel upload, the application submitted a moment ago, or a database error), the stored file is deleted again. Deleting a file removes the reference first and then the stored file (best effort); a failed delete is logged with the application and file id only, never a file name or any personal detail.

**Storage: Cloudinary, private.** Files are uploaded as type `authenticated` (no plain delivery URL exists), into the folder `tourist-visa/<applicationId>/`, with a random 32-character public id plus the extension of the detected type. **The original file name is never part of the storage id.** The resource type is **`raw`** for all three file types. Reason: a raw file is stored exactly as sent and never processed, and Cloudinary's account setting that restricts delivery of PDFs (and ZIPs) as images does not apply to raw files, so one path works for PDF, JPG and PNG alike. Delivery is a **signed download link with an expiry** (Cloudinary's `private_download_url` with `expires_at`), not a delivery URL: the signature covers the expiry, so it cannot be extended. The module is `backend/config/touristVisaStorage.js`; it passes its credentials with every call and does not change the global Cloudinary setup that the public assessment uploads use.

**Temporary links (admin only).** `touristVisaDocumentService.getTemporaryLink(applicationId, docId)` returns `{ url, expiresAt }` for a link that works for 2 minutes. It is **not reachable from any applicant route**. Since step 8e exactly one route calls it, `GET /api/tourist-visa/admin/applications/:id/documents/:docId/link`, behind the admin checks and with an audit row written first (section 2.3). It is covered by tests, including that no applicant path hands out a link.

**Data.** `documents[]` on the application now holds `id` (our own random id, the only identifier an applicant sees), `slot`, `publicId`, `resourceType`, `format`, `bytes`, `mime`, `uploadedAt` and `originalName`. **The original name is encrypted** with the field cipher, bound to the application and the document (`<applicationId>:documents.<docId>.originalName`), so a name cannot be moved to another file. The name is cleaned for display (no folders, control or direction-changing characters, at most 150 characters; an empty name becomes `document.<ext>`). The earlier `documents` shape (`kind`, `size`, `mimetype`) was never written by any code, so nothing needs migrating.

**Where it lives.** `backend/config/touristVisaDocuments.js` (limits, types, error codes), `backend/config/touristVisaStorage.js` (Cloudinary), `backend/services/touristVisaDocumentService.js`, `backend/controllers/touristVisaDocumentController.js`, `backend/middleware/touristVisaUpload.js` (multer and the rate limiter), `backend/utils/{detectFileType,documentError,touristVisaDocumentView}.js`, and the three routes in `backend/routes/touristVisaRoutes.js`. The repository gained the guarded add and remove.

**Parsers.** `touristVisaBodyParser` refuses any body that is not JSON; it now lets exactly `POST /applications/me/documents` through when the body is multipart, and multer reads it. The global JSON and URL-encoded parsers ignore multipart bodies, and the HTML sanitizer was already skipped for `/api/tourist-visa`. Every other Tourist Visa route still answers 415 to a multipart body. This is tested through the same middleware order as `index.js` and through the real `index.js`.

**What is not yet proven.** Neither step ran against real Cloudinary: the tests and the browser check use a fake storage, and the real adapter is tested for what it asks of the SDK and for the signed link's shape and expiry. One real upload of each type, one real signed download (with the developer tool above), the 2-minute expiry and one real delete must be done against the test Cloudinary account before the upload page is trusted; the steps are in section 14.4. The choice of `raw` is the one to watch: if Cloudinary refuses `authenticated` raw files on the account's plan, the fallback is `image` for JPG and PNG and `raw` for PDF, which is a change in `touristVisaStorage.js` only (the stored `resourceType` already travels with each file).

**Not covered here (security review, step 8j).** Files are not scanned for malware, and a PDF can contain active content: they must only ever be offered as downloads from the private link, never displayed inline from our own site. The rate limit is per server process.

### 2.2 Review and submit (step 8d)

**The applicant's flow.** The dashboard shows a "Review and submit" card. It is enabled only when every section that applies is complete and both documents are uploaded; otherwise it says what is still missing, section by section, with a link to each. "Review and submit" opens `/tourist-visa/review`: every part and section in order with an **Edit** link back to it, the files by name and size, the Part 9 ticks as given (with the date), then the **final declaration** (the wording of `declarations.submit`, with a Draft badge in preview mode), a required tick and a **Submit application** button. Submit opens the site's own dialog ("Submit your application? You will not be able to change it afterwards."); no native alert, confirm or prompt is used. The page can be visited by address at any time; while something is missing it shows the review and the list of what is missing, and Submit stays disabled. After a submit the page shows the **confirmation** (focus moves to its heading, and a live region and a toast announce it): the application's reference, "Submitted on <date>", "What happens next" and the declaration, followed by the same review with every edit control gone. The dashboard then shows the reference and a "View your application" link, with no Continue button and no section links.

**Read only after submit.** Any section address (`/tourist-visa/2A`, `/tourist-visa/10D` and so on) goes straight to the review page for a submitted application, never to a form. The server enforces it too: saving a section, uploading and deleting a file all answer the locked error (409), and a second submit answers 409 `ALREADY_SUBMITTED`. There is no withdraw or reopen: that is for an admin only (step 8f).

**One builder for every view: `shared/tourist-visa/view.js`** (with `view.d.ts`; synced to the client and the backend like the engine, and covered by the drift check). `buildReview(config, answers, documents, declarations)` is a pure function with no UI or server code in it. It returns the ordered parts, sections and rows: only questions that are visible, option labels instead of codes, dates written out ("1 March 2027"), amounts ("£1,500"), Yes and No, each repeating-group entry as its own numbered item ("Parent 1"), files as a name and a size, and a declaration tick as "Confirmed on <date>". Sensitive answers pass through as plain text, because the server decrypts before it calls the builder. It can never contain a link, a URL or a storage id: it is only ever given a file's name and size. The applicant's review page uses it now; **the admin Overview (8e) and the PDF (8h) must use the same function**, so the three always agree.

**The review is built on the server** (`GET /applications/me/review`), from the decrypted answers, and sent to the owner only. For a draft it uses the live config; for a submitted application it uses **the config it was submitted under** (below), so a later change to a question, an option label or the declaration wording never changes what the applicant sees of what they submitted. The answer carries `review`, `status`, `reference`, `submittedAt`, `configVersion`, `documentsComplete` and `declaration` (its wording and when it was accepted). The ordinary `GET /applications/me` also returns the status, `submittedAt` and the `reference`.

**The submit: `POST /applications/me/submit`**, body `{ "declarationAccepted": true }`, behind the feature gate and sign-in. The server first re-checks **everything against the current config**, and nothing is submitted unless all of it passes:

- every section that applies is saved and its stored answers still pass the rules (a section that was never saved, needs another look, or whose answers no longer validate counts);
- the documents are complete (`documentsComplete`: a file in every slot);
- every Part 9 tick still matches the current wording (a tick given for other words counts against its section);
- `declarationAccepted` is exactly `true`.

If anything fails, the answer is **422 `NOT_READY`** with `sections: [{ id, problems }]` (a section id and how many things in it need attention, in form order) and `declarationMissing`. It never contains an answer or a value. Other answers: 400 `INVALID_REQUEST` (the body is not exactly that shape), 404 `APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, 409 `ALREADY_SUBMITTED`, 409 `APPLICATION_CHANGED` (it changed three times while being submitted), 503 `REFERENCE_UNAVAILABLE` (no free reference found; nothing was submitted). The client turns each into plain words (`client/src/utils/touristVisaReview.ts`); for a 422 it lists the sections that need attention, each linking to its section.

**One atomic write.** draft -> submitted is a single conditional database write that matches only if the application is still the applicant's draft **and has not changed since it was checked** (it compares `updatedAt`), so what was checked is what is submitted. Of two submits at the same moment exactly one wins; the other is told 409 `ALREADY_SUBMITTED`. If the application changed between the check and the write, the server checks again (up to three times) instead of submitting something it did not check.

**What the submit records.** `status: submitted`, `submittedAt`, a **reference**, `configSha256` and `configVersion`, and `declarations.submit`: the same record as the Part 9 ticks (the SHA-256 of the exact wording of `declarations.submit`, the time, the user, the config version; **no IP address**). Nothing else changes: the answers, the files and the Part 9 records are left exactly as they were.

**The reference** is `TV-` and eight characters from `ABCDEFGHJKMNPQRSTUVWXYZ23456789` (31 characters: no 0, O, 1, I or L, so it can be read out over the phone), for example `TV-K7M2QX9P`: about 850 billion possibilities, drawn with `crypto.randomInt`. It has a **unique index** (applications without one are not in it); if a reference is already taken the server draws another (up to five times, then answers 503 and submits nothing). The reference is a label to quote, not a secret and not a credential: nothing is ever looked up by it from the applicant side.

**The config snapshot.** The exact `questions.json` the application was submitted under is stored as text in its own collection, `touristvisaconfigsnapshots`, with the SHA-256 of that text as its `_id` (plus `version` and `createdAt`). It is written once per distinct config (an upsert that only inserts), never changed, and holds only the form's questions: no answer and nothing about an applicant. The application stores `configSha256` and `configVersion`. **Later views (the admin Overview, the PDF) must render a submitted application with its snapshot** (`touristVisaApplicationService.configOf(application)` does this; it falls back to the live config only for a draft) and must not use the live config for a submitted application.

**Logging.** A submit writes one log line at most (in development only) with the application's id and nothing else; a refusal's response and the log carry section ids and counts only, never an answer, and a database error is turned into a plain 500 by the same log-safe handler as the other routes.

**Approved wording.** The final declaration (`declarations.submit`) and the steps shown after submitting (`afterSubmit`) are our own plain words, kept in `questions.json` and approved as final by the senior on **2026-11-01**. The draft notes were removed without touching the words, so **the wording hash of every Part 9 tick and of the final declaration is exactly what it was**: every tick and declaration already given stays valid (a test pins the five hashes, section 8). The release check also proves the review page can be built from the config.

**Not built in this step:** the admin screens (built in 8e, section 2.3), withdrawing or reopening (8f, admin only), the cover letter (8g), the PDF (8h) and a confirmation email.

### 2.3 The admin side: list, Overview, files, status and activity (step 8e)

Read only, plus a status. **Correcting answers, notes, calls and flags were added in step 8f and are described in section 2.4**; this section describes what step 8e built, with the small changes 8f made to it noted in place.

**Who gets in.** Every route is under `/api/tourist-visa/admin/*` and sits behind, in this order: the feature switch (the same fixed 404 `FEATURE_CLOSED` as every other route while `TOURIST_VISA_ENABLED` is not `true`), `noStore`, `isAuthorized` (no or a bad token: 401) and `isAdmin` (a signed-in user who is not an admin: 403). The role is read from the database on every request (`role` is the number `1`), so taking the role away from an account works at once, even for a token that was already issued. **Every admin answer, including the refusals, carries `Cache-Control: no-store`.** Errors are log-safe like the rest of the feature: the response and the log hold a code, never an answer, a name, a file name or a link.

| Route | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /admin/applications` | The list. Query: `status` (`submitted`, `in_review` or `completed`), `flag` (one of the five admin flags, 8f), `q` (search, at most 100 characters), `page`, `pageSize` (default 20, at most 50). A bad value is 400 `INVALID_QUERY`. |
| `GET /admin/applications/:id` | The Overview. |
| `GET /admin/applications/:id/documents/:docId/link` | A link to one file that works for 2 minutes. |
| `PATCH /admin/applications/:id/status` | Moves the status. Body `{ "status": "in_review" }`. |
| `GET /admin/applications/:id/activity` | The activity log of one application, newest first (up to 100). |

**Drafts are invisible to admins.** The list never contains a draft, and a draft's id, an unknown id and an id that is not an id all answer the same 404 `APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`. Only `submitted`, `in_review` and `completed` applications can be listed, opened, linked or moved.

**The list.** Newest submission first. A row holds the id, the reference, the applicant's name, email and telephone number, `submittedAt`, the status, the number of files and (8f) the admin flags. Search is a case-insensitive match: a text of the shape `TV-` plus eight letters or digits is matched as a whole **reference**; anything else is matched as "contains" against the name and the email. **The typed text is escaped before it is used**, so `.*` or `(` match only themselves, and the query is a plain string (never an object). The list is one aggregation that counts and pages in the same query.

**The summary on the application.** To keep the list from decrypting or scanning answers, the application carries a small copy of who the applicant is: `summary: { name, email, phone }` (name is "given family", email in lower case, phone as "+44 7700 900123"). It is written **at submit** by the same write that submits. An application submitted before 8e has none: the list fills it in **lazily, once**, the first time it is listed or opened (at most 50 per request, and the write only happens if `summary` does not exist yet, so it can never overwrite one). **These four answers (2.1, 2.2, 2.4 and 2.32) are not encrypted fields**, so the summary stores no plain copy of a secret; it is a second plain copy of data that is already stored plain. If any of them is ever made an encrypted field, the summary must stop storing it. **Step 8f refreshes the summary in the same transaction whenever an admin corrects one of those answers** (section 2.4), so the list never shows an old name, email or number.

**The Overview.** Built on the server from the decrypted answers with the shared `buildReview` (section 2.2), using **the config snapshot the application was submitted under** (`configOf`), never the live config. It returns:

- `application`: id, reference, status, `submittedAt`, `configVersion` and `allowedMoves` (the status moves open from here);
- `call`: the **call block** for the first phone call: name, telephone (shown, and as a `tel:` value), email, **preferred language** (10.4, or the language typed at 10.4a for "Other"), the contact preferences (where the applicant is, the kind of number, how they want to be contacted: 2.33, 2.34, 2.35 and the email check 2.9), the reference, `submittedAt` and the status;
- `review`: every part and section as the applicant gave it, in words;
- `documents`: each file's id, slot, **name**, size and type, and `fileSlots` (which file question each slot belongs to);

and **never a link, a URL, a storage id or an encrypted value**. The decrypted answers go to admins only, and only through this route.

**Files.** The page shows each file by name and size with an **Open** button. Pressing it asks the server for `/documents/:docId/link`, which calls the existing `documentService.getTemporaryLink` (the storage's signed link, **2 minutes**); that is **the only way a link leaves the server**. The page opens the link in a new tab straight away, does not show or keep it, and does not log it. If the browser blocks the new tab, a link appears next to the file for 90 seconds. A file that is not on the application is 404 `DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND`.

**Status.** Only these moves are allowed: `submitted` to `in_review`, `in_review` to `completed`, and `completed` back to `in_review`. Anything else is 409 `INVALID_STATUS_MOVE`, a body that is not exactly `{ "status": <one of the three> }` is 400 (`INVALID_REQUEST`, `INVALID_STATUS`), and a status that changed between the check and the write is 409 `STATUS_CHANGED` (the write is conditional on the status the move started from, so two admins cannot both win). A draft cannot be moved. The page asks first, in the site's own dialog ("Start reviewing this application?" and so on); there is no native confirm. The status is not a lock: the applicant side already treats every non-draft as read only.

**The audit log: collection `touristvisaaudit`** (model `TouristVisaAudit`). One row for each of:

| `action` | Written when | Extra fields |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `list_view` | An application was shown on a page of the list. **One row for each application on the page**, so a page of 20 writes 20 rows (a default we chose: the alternative is one row per list request). | none |
| `overview_view` | The Overview was opened. Exactly one row for each view. | none |
| `document_link` | A file link was requested. | `documentId` (the id only, never the name, the link or the storage id) |
| `status_change` | The status was moved. | `fromStatus`, `toStatus` |

Every row has `adminId`, `applicationId`, `action` and `at`. **A row never holds an answer, a name, a file name, a link or an IP address**, and a test checks the exact set of fields and scans the stored rows for values. **Fail closed:** the audit row is written *before* the data leaves the server, and if it cannot be written the admin gets a plain 500 and no data (for a status change the move is undone). **Append only, as far as the application can make it so:** the model refuses every update and delete operation and every save of an existing row; the repository has two functions, `append` and `findByApplication`; and no route can change or remove a row. **The database itself does not enforce this** (anyone with write access to the collection can still change it): see section 14.5. Reading the activity log is **not** itself audited.

**The pages** (admin area, same layout and menu as the other admin pages):

- `/admin/tourist-visa`: a table on wide screens and one card per application on a phone, a search box, a status filter, Clear, previous and next with the page count, and loading, empty and error states. A result count is announced to screen readers.
- `/admin/tourist-visa/<id>`: the call block first (a large `tel:` button, a Copy number button, language, contact preferences, reference, date, status), the status buttons, a jump list to every section and to Activity, the whole application, and the Activity panel (who opened the Overview, a file, or changed the status, and when).
- **The menu entry "Tourist Visa" appears only when the feature is open** (the server says open and this build's own hold passes, section 4.2): not while the answer is pending and not when it is closed. When it is not open, both pages show the same "This application is not available yet" notice (with a link back to the admin area) and ask the API for nothing.
- The pages reuse the applicant's `ReviewSummary` (with an optional way to draw the files), the site's `ConfirmDialog` and toasts, and add no native alert, confirm or prompt.

**Not in step 8e (8f built the first three, section 2.4):** editing answers, notes and flags, and a "corrected" marker. Still to build: the cover letter (8g), the PDF (8h), a confirmation email, admin MFA, and retention of the audit log (section 14.5).

### 2.4 Correcting answers, notes, the call log and flags (step 8f)

An administrator can correct the answers of a **submitted** application, keep notes and a call log, and flag it. Everything is behind the same chain as section 2.3 (feature switch, `noStore`, `isAuthorized`, `isAdmin`), every answer carries `Cache-Control: no-store`, and every read of decrypted answers, of the history or of the notes is written to the audit log **before** it is returned.

| Route | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /admin/applications/:id/edit-form` | What the Edit forms need: the **config the application was submitted under**, its decrypted answers and its `revision`. Audited as `edit_open`. A completed application is 409. |
| `PUT /admin/applications/:id/changes` | Body `{ expectedRevision, reason, changes, dryRun? }`. A dry run previews; a real call corrects. |
| `GET /admin/applications/:id/changes` | The history, newest first, with the reason and the old and new values in words. Audited as `changes_view`. |
| `GET`, `POST /admin/applications/:id/notes` | The notes and the call log; add a note or a call. Audited as `notes_view` and `notes_add`. |
| `PUT /admin/applications/:id/flags` | Body `{ flags: [...] }`. Audited as `flags_change` with the old and the new list. |

There is still **no route that updates or deletes** an audit row, a change record or a note.

**The revision.** Every application has a numeric `revision` (0 for every application saved before 8f: a missing field counts as 0). Every successful correction adds one. The request quotes the revision the admin was looking at (`expectedRevision`); if it is not the current one the answer is **409 `REVISION_CHANGED`** and nothing is written, so two admins cannot overwrite each other. The write itself is conditional on the revision too, so of two corrections sent at the same moment with the same revision exactly one wins and the other is told 409.

**When it is allowed.** Only while the status is `submitted` or `in_review`. A completed application answers **409 `STATUS_NOT_EDITABLE`** (reopen it first); a draft is not visible to an admin at all, so it answers the same 404 `APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND` as an unknown id (8e's rule, kept).

**The request.** `changes` is a list of at most **30** items `{ questionId, index?, value }`; `value` null clears an answer. `questionId` is a question, with `index` (the entry number, from 0) for an answer inside a repeating group's entry. A repeating group can also be sent **as a whole** (`questionId` is the group's id, no `index`, `value` the list of entries): that is how entries are added or removed. Entries must be added one after the other. `reason` is required for a real correction: **10 to 1000 characters after trimming**. A dry run does **not** need one (the reason is asked for after the preview has been read) and never keeps one. Any other field in the body is refused.

**What an admin cannot change**, each with its own 400 code: the Part 9 ticks (`NOT_EDITABLE_DECLARATION`, as is `declarations.submit`), the documents (`NOT_EDITABLE_DOCUMENTS`, also any file question), `status` (`NOT_EDITABLE_STATUS`: use the status buttons), `reference`, `submittedAt` and who the application belongs to (`NOT_EDITABLE_REFERENCE`, `NOT_EDITABLE_SUBMITTED_AT`, `NOT_EDITABLE_IDENTITY`). A question that does not exist is 400 `UNKNOWN_QUESTION`. The applicant's *answers* about themselves (name, email, telephone) **can** be corrected; the account itself cannot.

**How it is checked** (`backend/utils/touristVisaChanges.js`, pure functions, tested without a database):

- It uses **the config snapshot the application was submitted under** (`configOf`), never today's config, for validation and for the words in the preview.
- The same shared engine as a normal save checks **every section the change touches, as a whole** (so changing the email alone fails while the email choices of 2A still name the old one).
- Answers the change **hides are pruned** from the whole application (including whole repeating groups) and listed separately.
- Answers that become **newly required must be supplied in the same request**: a section the change makes necessary elsewhere (single to married needs 4A), a follow-up (other names Yes needs an entry), or a rule it newly breaks in a section it did not touch, is refused. A problem that was *already* there in a section the request did not touch (a date rule that has lapsed with time) is **not** blamed on the change.
- Refusal: **422 `CHANGES_INVALID`** with `sections: [{ id, problems }]` and `errors` (the engine's own fixed messages per field, for example "Enter a valid answer"). **Never a value.**

**Dry run.** `dryRun: true` runs everything above and returns `{ dryRun: true, revision, sections, changes, removed, required }` and writes **nothing except one audit row** (`changes_preview`, no values). Each line is `{ sectionId, sectionTitle, questionId, index?, number, label, old, new }` with the values written by the shared view builder (`describeValue`: "1 March 2027", "£1,500", an option's label). `changes` are the answers set or cleared (an added entry shows "New entry" as its old value), `removed` the answers removed because they became hidden, and `required` the questions the change made necessary together with what was supplied for them. A dry run that does not pass writes no audit row.

**The real correction is one database transaction** (`touristVisaChangeRepository.applyEdit`): it checks the revision and the status, updates the answers (the sensitive ones re-encrypted with the field cipher, bound to this application and question, see section 2 "Encryption"), adds one to `revision`, refreshes the `summary` (name, email, telephone) when those answers changed, inserts the change record and inserts the `changes_apply` audit row. If anything fails **nothing is written** (tests make the record and the audit row fail). **This needs a MongoDB that supports transactions: a replica set. Atlas always is; a standalone local MongoDB answers 503 `TRANSACTIONS_UNAVAILABLE`.** The tests and the browser check use an in-memory replica set.

**The change record: collection `touristvisachanges`** (model `TouristVisaChange`), append only in the same way as the audit log (the model refuses every update and delete, the repository has `applyEdit` and reads only): `applicationId`, `adminId`, `at`, `revisionBefore`, `revisionAfter`, `reason` and `changes`. Every item is `{ sectionId, questionId, index?, kind, old?, new? }`; `kind` is `edited` (set or cleared by the admin), `pruned` (removed because the change hid it) or `structure` (a repeating group got more or fewer entries; old and new are the counts). **The reason and the old and new value of every item are encrypted** with the field cipher, bound to the record and the item (`touristvisachanges:<record id>:<item>.old`), **whether or not the question is itself a sensitive one**. The ids, section, kind and times are plain. A test scans the database for every value, reason and note used and finds none.

**The "corrected by our team" marker** (applicant-visible). The shared review builder takes an optional fifth argument, `corrections`: a map from a *correction key* to a time. The key is the question id, `questionId[index]` for an answer inside a repeating group's entry (for example `q4_8b[1]`), or the group's id when its entries were added or removed (which also forgets the older entry marks of that group, because the numbers shifted). A marked row gets `correctedByTeam`: **the date in words and nothing else**, so the applicant's review and the read-only page show "Corrected by our team on 5 October 2026". It carries **no old value and no reason**, and answers that were *pruned* are not shown at all. The markers are read from the change records **without their values** (`findCorrectionMarks` selects only ids, kinds and times). The applicant's `GET /applications/me` carries them as `corrections` (key to time) for a submitted application, and `GET /applications/me/review` as `correctedByTeam` on the rows; both are empty for an application nobody corrected. The admin Overview shows the same marker. Nothing else of this step ever reaches an applicant response: not the revision, the reason, the old values, the change record, the notes, the call log or the flags (a test scans every applicant response).

**Notes and the call log: collection `touristvisanotes`** (model `TouristVisaNote`), append only: `applicationId`, `adminId`, `at`, `kind` (`note` or `call`), `text` (**encrypted**, bound to the note's own id, at most 2000 characters) and for a call an `outcome` (`reached`, `no_answer`, `wrong_number`, `call_back`). A note needs words; a call needs an outcome and may have none. Body `{ kind, text, outcome? }`, anything else is 400. Who wrote it always comes from the signed-in admin. Adding is audited **first** (`notes_add`), then written.

**Flags.** `adminFlags` on the application: any of `needs_call`, `called`, `awaiting_applicant`, `verified`, `concern`, kept in that fixed order. They are admin only and are in no applicant response. Changing them is audited as `flags_change` with the old and the new list (the words are not sensitive); a change whose audit row cannot be written is undone. The list route has a `flag` filter and shows each row's flags.

**The audit log**, additions to the table in section 2.3: `edit_open`, `changes_preview`, `changes_apply`, `changes_view`, `notes_view`, `notes_add` (ids only) and `flags_change` (`fromFlags`, `toFlags`). A row still never holds an answer, a reason, a note, a file name, a link or an address.

**The pages.** On the Overview (`/admin/tourist-visa/<id>`):

- Each section has an **Edit** button (none on the files section; none while the application is completed, where a note says why). It opens the **same field components the applicant form uses**, filled with the current answers and built from the snapshot config; the Part 9 ticks are shown, disabled, with a note.
- The flow is **Review changes** (a dry run) -> a diff of old and new values, the answers that will be removed and the questions that now apply -> a **Reason** box (10 to 1000, a counter) -> **Apply changes** -> the site's own confirm dialog -> applied. Several sections can be edited in one go (a chip for each, and a "Edit another section" list); a 422 lists the sections to fix, each as a button, marks the fields, and a 409 offers **Load the latest version**. Cancel with unsaved changes asks first. After a save the Overview reloads, the changed rows say "Corrected by our team", and the **Changes** panel lists the history.
- **Notes and calls**: add a note or log a call (four outcome buttons), newest first. **Flags**: five toggle buttons that save at once. The list page has a flag filter and shows each row's flags.
- 375 px, light and dark, keyboard and screen-reader use, focus management (to the editor's heading on open, to the diff, back to the section's Edit button on close) and the site's own dialogs and toasts; no native alert, confirm or prompt.

**Things we decided that you may want to change.** (1) A dry run needs no reason, because the reason is asked for after the preview; a real correction needs one. (2) `CHANGES_INVALID` carries the engine's fixed field messages as well as the section counts. (3) `pruned` answers are kept (encrypted) in the change record so nothing an admin's change removes is lost. (4) The old value of an answer is shown to the admin in the preview and in the Changes panel; it is never shown to the applicant. (5) Restricting the *number of entries* of a repeating group follows the engine's rules (a count-driven group has exactly the number its count question says). (6) Leaving the page with unsaved edits is not guarded by the browser's own prompt (we use no native dialogs); the editor's own Cancel asks.

**Rule for step 8g (the cover letter).** A correction that touches an answer the cover letter uses **must create a new letter version**. The change records say which answers each correction touched (`changes[].questionId`, with `kind` and `revisionAfter`), so the letter step can compare them with the answers its template reads; a letter edited by hand is kept in the history as before.

### 2.5 The currency helper (config 1.1.0)

A small inline tool under every money question, so an applicant who thinks in another currency (usually Pakistani rupees) gets the pound figure without leaving the page. It only fills the field with a whole number of pounds, as if the applicant had typed it. **Nothing from the helper is saved**: not the currency, the amount typed or the rate. The stored answer is `{ amount, currency: "GBP" }`, exactly as before, and the normal validation applies to it.

**Config.** An optional boolean `currencyHelper` on a question. `validateConfig` accepts it **only on money (`AMT`) questions**, only as a boolean, and, when the question lists accepted currencies, only where `GBP` is one of them (the helper fills in pounds). It is `true` on all ten money questions: 2.31e (inside the medical treatments), 5.1e, 5.1j, 5.7, 6.1a, 6.2, 6.3, 6.4, 6.5f (inside the payers) and 6.8e (inside the public funds received). No wording, option, rule or id changed, so the version is `1.1.0`; a script proved that the old and the new config differ in nothing but the version and the flag, and the wording hash of the four Part 9 ticks and of `declarations.submit` is unchanged (the test that pins the hashes still passes).

**The admin Edit form and older applications.** The Edit form is built from the config an application was *submitted under*, which for an application submitted before `1.1.0` has no flag. A question therefore gets the helper when its own config says so **or**, if it does not say anything, when today's config flags the same question id (and it is still a money question): `wantsCurrencyHelper` in `client/src/utils/touristVisaCurrency.ts`. The helper is a convenience beside the field, not part of the submitted wording or data, so this does not break the rule that a submitted application is shown with its own snapshot. A question whose snapshot says `currencyHelper: false` never gets it. This rule is covered by unit tests and a source check on `QuestionField`; the automated browser check below used an application submitted under `1.1.0`.

**The route.** `GET /api/tourist-visa/rates` answers `{ base: "GBP", asOf, rates, stale, attribution }`, with `rates` = units of each currency for 1 GBP, `asOf` the date of the rates and `attribution` a link the page must show (below) or `null`. It sits **behind the feature switch** (a closed deployment answers the fixed 404 `FEATURE_CLOSED`), **behind `isAuthorized`** (sign-in; an admin and an applicant may both call it) and **behind the site's general limiter** (100 requests per 15 minutes per IP). It sends `Cache-Control: private, max-age=300`. It sends nothing about the caller anywhere: the providers are asked the same fixed question whoever is asking, and no input of the caller reaches a URL.

**Currencies.** Only these 29 codes are ever returned (`ALLOWED_CURRENCIES` in `backend/config/touristVisaRates.js`): PKR, USD, EUR, AED, SAR, INR, CAD, AUD, CNY, JPY, TRY, QAR, KWD, OMR, BHD, BDT, LKR, NPR, MYR, SGD, CHF, NOK, SEK, DKK, ZAR, NZD, EGP, KES, NGN. A code the provider does not return is simply left out. **PKR must be present**: an answer without it counts as that provider having failed.

**Providers, in this order** (fixed URLs in the same file; nothing is built from user input):

1. **Frankfurter**, `https://api.frankfurter.dev`. European Central Bank rates.
2. **Exchange Rate API (open access)**, `https://open.er-api.com/v6/latest/GBP`. **Its terms require a visible link "Rates By Exchange Rate API" to https://www.exchangerate-api.com** wherever its rates are shown. The route returns `attribution` **only when this provider supplied the data**, otherwise `null`, and the helper shows the link whenever it is given.

**What we found with a live check** (one request to each provider on 2026-11-03, from outside the repository, nothing stored, no key): **Frankfurter returned 16 of the 29 codes and no PKR**, so under the PKR rule it never answers and **open.er-api.com is the provider that answers in practice** (all 29, PKR about 371 per pound on that day). In practice the "Rates By Exchange Rate API" link is therefore always shown. If you want rupees without that provider, a different source of PKR is needed. The first provider is kept because its answer is used if it ever adds PKR.

**Every provider call.** GET only, a 5-second timeout, a body of at most 64 KB (read as a stream and stopped at the limit), redirects followed by hand and only to the same host (at most 2), the answer checked strictly: base is GBP, a valid date, every allowed rate a finite number above 0 (one bad rate fails the whole answer), PKR present. A provider's error, body or status text is **never** passed on to the applicant and never logged: a failed provider logs one short fixed line with its name (`Tourist Visa rates provider failed`), and all providers failing logs one fixed error line.

**Cache and stale rules** (`CACHE` in the same config file; in memory, per server process): good rates are kept **12 hours**. After that the next request refreshes them; if every provider fails, the **last good rates are served for up to 7 days marked `stale: true`**; after 7 days the route answers **503 `RATES_UNAVAILABLE`** with a fixed message. After a failed refresh the providers are not asked again for **5 minutes**, and simultaneous requests share one refresh. A restart empties the cache.

**The helper in the browser** (`client/src/components/tourist-visa/CurrencyHelper.tsx`, the logic in `client/src/utils/touristVisaCurrency.ts`, the shared fetch in `client/src/hooks/useTouristVisaRates.ts`). It is drawn by `QuestionField`, so it appears in the applicant form, inside repeating groups and in the admin Edit form. A link-style button "Enter in another currency" opens a currency choice (PKR first, the rest alphabetical), an amount box (digits and one decimal point, at most 12 digits, no minus), and a live line such as "About £1,250 (1 GBP = 380 PKR, rates of 3 November 2026)" with "Approximate. The component uses only the site's own components (no native dialog) and works at 375 px, in light and dark, by keyboard and with a screen reader.

### 2.6 The AI-assisted cover letter (step 8g)

An administrator can have a cover letter written for a submitted application, check and edit it, and approve it. **Admin only**: the applicant never sees it, and there is no PDF yet (step 8h). The senior wants an AI, the administrator reviews and edits every letter, and **submitting an application never depends on the AI**: the letter is written on demand by an administrator, never automatically at submit and never from the browser.

**The principles.** (1) Generated on demand, by a button, by an administrator. (2) A deterministic **template letter** is the fallback whenever the AI is off, failing, rate limited or over a limit, and a letter can always be written or edited by hand. (3) Parts 9 and 10A, the free text of 10B, health and treatment, licence and reference numbers, documents and every direct identifier **never** feed the letter.

**Layers of fallback**, in the order a request meets them. A fixed code is recorded for the reason, never any text.

1. The AI is off (`AI_DISABLED`), not set up (`AI_NOT_CONFIGURED`), or in production without the data-terms confirmation (`AI_TERMS_NOT_CONFIRMED`) -> template.
2. The circuit breaker is open (`AI_CIRCUIT_OPEN`), the daily limit (`AI_DAILY_LIMIT`) or the administrator's hourly limit (`AI_ADMIN_LIMIT`) is reached -> template; the provider is not asked.
3. The provider is asked and fails: rate limited (`AI_RATE_LIMITED`), too slow (`AI_TIMEOUT`), unreachable (`AI_NETWORK`), refuses the request, for example a wrong key (`AI_PROVIDER_REJECTED`), errors (`AI_PROVIDER_ERROR`), answers with something that is not a letter (`AI_BAD_RESPONSE`) or is cut off (`AI_TRUNCATED`) -> template.
4. The AI answers but the letter uses banned wording even after **one automatic second request** (`AI_BANNED_PHRASE`, below), or fails the other checks below (`LETTER_EMPTY`, `LETTER_LENGTH`, `LETTER_LINK`, `LETTER_UNKNOWN_TOKEN`, `LETTER_MISSING_TOKEN`, `LETTER_NUMBER_NOT_IN_FACTS`, `LETTER_DATE_NOT_IN_FACTS`) -> template.

The administrator is shown a friendly sentence for each ("The AI was not available, so a template letter was created."). A fallback is a normal `201`, not an error.

**The privacy design: one allowlist.** `shared/tourist-visa/coverLetterFacts.js` (copied to the client and the backend by `npm run sync:shared`, with `coverLetterFacts.d.ts`) maps specific question ids to the facts the letter may use. A question that is not named there can never reach the letter, whether the AI or the template writes it.

- `validateAllowlist` refuses any id that is in Parts 9, 10A, 10B, 10C or 10D, in sections 2A, 2D, 3 or 4A, stored encrypted, of type email, telephone, file or declaration, in the excluded list below, or an address read in any way but its town or its country. `buildFacts` runs it every time and **refuses to build** (`FACTS_UNSAFE`, then a 500 `COVER_LETTER_UNAVAILABLE`) if it finds a problem, so a widened allowlist cannot slip into production silently.
- Tests prove that none of those ids is on the allowlist and that **adding any of them makes validation fail** (`shared/tourist-visa/coverLetterFacts.test.js`).
- People are **counted**, never named. Hidden answers (a gate answered No, a status that does not apply) are not used even if they are still stored.
- A medical visit (1.1 is Medical): the reason, the holiday kind and the applicant's own words about the purpose (1.5) are **not sent**, because they can describe treatment. The letter then has a `[not provided: main reason for the visit]` gap for the administrator to write by hand.

**Placeholders and free text.** The facts sent use `[NAME]`, `[EMPLOYER]` and `[SCHOOL]`. The model is told to use them verbatim and the server puts the real values in **after** the letter is written (`substitute`). A bracketed token that is not one of the three, or whose value the facts did not supply, or a letter that lacks `[NAME]`, fails the generation (template fallback). Free text that is on the allowlist (1.5, 5.1d, 5.8, 5.6 and 5.1h; **6.7y is no longer free text**, see "City and type of stay" below) goes to the AI inside `<data>` blocks (with a statement that anything in them is data, never an instruction), after **redaction with tokens that remember the original text**:

- Whatever must not reach the AI is taken out of the applicant's own words and replaced by a token `[R1]`, `[R2]`, ...: the applicant's name (the whole name, and each part of it, also each side of a hyphen or apostrophe), the employer's, the business's and the school's name, emails, telephone numbers, links, and any text in **square brackets** (so nothing they type can look like one of our placeholders). Each token remembers **exactly what was there, as it was typed** (letters and case): "Test" and "test" are two tokens. The same text shares a token.
- After the letter is written (and has passed the checks) the server puts the original text back, in **one pass** (`substitute` with the redactions): `[NAME]`, `[EMPLOYER]` and `[SCHOOL]` become the real values, and each `[R#]` becomes exactly the text it replaced, never the full name or the full employer name. A restored text is not searched again, so nothing it contains is mistaken for a token. The template letter goes through the same substitution.
- **The reproduction that was fixed**: first name "Test", job description "I test software for a company ... writing test plans". The old redaction replaced the word with the name placeholder and the substitution wrote the full name back ("I Test Person software"). It now comes back exactly as written, and the AI never sees the word. A test with exactly this text runs the real service, the template and the facts; others cover names that are ordinary words (Test, Will, Hope, Mark, Grace, Rose, Bill, Art) with several occurrences, other cases, possessives and longer words (not matched), names in Arabic, Cyrillic and Chinese script, accents, overlapping names and tokens that collide.
- A name is matched **as a whole word**, in any case, except in scripts where that does not work (Chinese, Japanese, Thai and the like, and Arabic and Hebrew, where a name takes attached prefixes): there it is matched anywhere. Matching too much is safe because everything is put back exactly. Single letters count as a name only in scripts where one character is a name part.
- The token must be one the facts made: a `[R#]` the AI invents fails the generation (`LETTER_UNKNOWN_TOKEN`, template fallback). What was taken out is part of what the facts are made of, so it is part of the change hash.
- **What is still lost on purpose**: markup (angle brackets and tags), repeated spaces and anything past 500 characters. Nothing else of the applicant's words is changed by the round trip.
- The AI may still reword or leave out what the applicant wrote (the prompt asks it to reword); the round trip only guarantees that what it keeps from them, token for token, comes back as written.
- Because emails, telephone numbers and links come back too, **a contact detail the applicant typed in a free-text box can appear in the letter** if the AI uses its token. The letter is admin only, and the administrator reads it before approving.
- **This does not catch a stranger's name typed into a free-text box without a title**, and an employer's name written only in part is not recognised (see 14.5). A best-effort scrub of postcodes, house numbers with streets and titled names is part of the same round trip (below).

It is therefore **no longer sent as text**. The fact `accommodationArea` is of the kind `places`: only the well-known places of a **fixed list of about 100** (`PLACES` in `shared/tourist-visa/places.js`, shared by both builders; the function `placesIn` returns list names only, never any of the text) that the answer names are sent, as `Area or city they expect to stay in: London`. If it names none, **nothing is sent for the area**: the type of stay and the dates still go, the template says "the United Kingdom" (the cover letter says nothing about where, the itinerary says "Arrive in the United Kingdom"), and the prompts say to use only the facts and leave a missing one out, so **a city is never invented**. A town that is not on the list is therefore not sent from 6.7y (it is still sent from the town of an address, 6.7b, below). The type of stay (6.7x) is said in words of ours: **"Other" is "somewhere else"**, never the bare word (the itinerary says all five options in its own words, the cover letter only maps "Other" and keeps its other wording). The town of an address (6.7b) is read as a town: **a town with a digit, a comma or more than 60 characters is an address typed in the wrong box and is dropped**, and the rest goes through the scrub below. **The best-effort scrub of free text** (`GENERIC_PATTERNS` and `TITLE_PASS` in `coverLetterFacts.js`, applied by `cleanFreeText` to every free text before it reaches either AI). It runs in **the same redaction round trip** as the applicant's name, the employer, emails, telephone numbers, links and square brackets: each match is replaced by **its own token that remembers exactly what was typed**, and the server puts the original back after the text is written, so the letter (and the template) still holds **the applicant's own words exactly**. It matches:

- **UK postcodes** (`SW1A 1AA`, `m1 1ae`, any spacing);
- **a flat** (`Flat 4`, `Apartment 9`, `Unit 12b`);
- **a house number, up to three words and a street word** (road, rd, street, st, avenue, ave, lane, ln, drive, dr, close, way, court, ct, gardens, gdns, place, pl, terrace, crescent, grove, square, sq, mews, parade, boulevard): `12 Acacia Road`, `3-5 High St`, `7 St Peters Road`; the words between cannot be function words or counts (`in`, `a`, `for`, `nights`, `people`...), so "Room for 2 in a quiet place" is left alone;
- **a title and the one or two capitalised words after it** (Mr, Mrs, Ms, Miss, Mx, Dr, Prof, Professor, in capitals too): `Mr Ahmed Khan`, `Dr. Alan Grant`, `Ms Chloë Dubois-Martin`. A title followed by a lowercase word ("miss the train", "Mr and Mrs") is not a name and is left alone.

What it leaves alone, by test: place names ("Bath", "Edinburgh", "Lake District"), ordinary words, dates and amounts ("15 March 2027", "3,500"), "Kensington High Street" without a number, and text in other scripts (Arabic, Hebrew, Chinese, Japanese, Russian, Greek, Thai, Hindi), which is never corrupted. A false positive costs the AI a little context (a token where a phrase was) but never the applicant's words, which always come back exactly. **This is best effort, not a guarantee**: it catches the common shapes only (the list under "what can still get through" above), so **the administrator must still read every letter and itinerary before approving, and the production guard (a paid or zero-retention provider and its terms, `AI_DATA_TERMS_CONFIRMED`) is what covers the residual risk**, not this code. The tests that prove it: each pattern in several spellings, the cases that must not be scrubbed, non-Latin and mixed text, the whole round trip returning the original text unchanged (with the applicant's name and a typed `[R1]` in it), and the scrub through both builders and both services.

**The facts hashes changed, so every earlier letter and itinerary shows the stale banner once.** The keyed hash of the facts now has version 2 (`touristvisacoverletter:facts:v2:` and `touristvisaitinerary:facts:v2:`; it was `v1`). A version written before this change is flagged "Answers used by the letter (itinerary) have changed." once, its Approve is off, and a new version (generated, or an edit saved) clears it; nothing is regenerated automatically and the old version stays in the history. After that it is stale only when an answer it uses changes. A test pins the prefixes, and the browser check writes a version with the old hash and watches the banner appear once and not return.

**The anti-hallucination check** (`utils/touristVisaCoverLetterChecks.js`), after generation and before saving: every date, number and amount in the letter, and every number written out in words (two to a million), must also be in the facts the model was given, after formatting ("£1,250" is "1250"); a length of 150 to 700 words (the prompt asks for 300 to 450); no link or markup. It checks the **set** of numbers, not their place: a figure that appears somewhere in the facts passes anywhere in the letter. A failure returns a fixed code and none of the text.

**The prompt** is in one file, `backend/config/touristVisaCoverLetterPrompt.js`: a formal first-person British English cover letter for a UK Standard Visitor visa application, 300 to 450 words, only from the facts, no invented details, no promises about the outcome, no legal advice, placeholders verbatim. **Prompt version 3** (the current one) keeps the rules of version 2 and adds the rules in the second list below. **Prompt version 2** added these rules, from what the first manual test showed:

- **The applicant's own words** (the `<data>` blocks) are worked into ordinary sentences. Never announced ("in my own words", "as I have expressed", "I have described"), never in quotation marks, and left out if empty, meaningless or awkward.
- **No conclusions and no judgements.** The letter states supplied facts and makes one closing request, and nothing else: no statement that the applicant complies with, meets or satisfies a rule, no enthusiasm, no "demonstrating", "illustrating" or "underscoring", no "strong ties" or "stable situation", no remark about funds or public funds, no promise or guarantee (including a return). It may only say what a supplied fact says.
- **People are described naturally** from the counts and relationships given, and nothing is written about the form or about details "having been provided". The only references to the application are the opening sentence and the closing request.
- The labels the AI is shown were reworded so they do not teach the phrases above ("Further information about the visit", "Number of parents", "About the job"), and the length of employment no longer says "at the time of the application". The facts' change hash covers the values, not the wording of labels, so rewording a label never makes a letter stale. (The first time this version runs, a letter written before it shows as stale once: the facts' wording changed. Generate a new version.)

**Prompt version 3** adds, from the second manual test (real text, a first name that is an ordinary word):

- **Tone: formal and human.** A thoughtful person writing carefully to a visa officer: polite, plain and precise British English, varied sentence length, natural connecting phrases; not stiff or robotic; no flattery, marketing language or filler; no bullet points or headings. Strictly factual: every earlier rule on claims still applies.
- **Originality.** Every letter is written freshly from the facts. **There is no sample letter, model paragraph or stock phrasing in the prompt** (the few quoted phrases are ones the model must not write, or the greeting and closing; a test checks this), and the model is told not to reuse boilerplate or well-known sample cover letter wording, to choose the order and structure of the paragraphs that suit the facts so that two applicants with similar facts do not get near-identical letters, and not to copy the applicant's own text word for word beyond a short phrase. **Nothing in the prompt, the code or the tests is meant to hide that an AI helped**: the administrator reads, edits and approves every letter, the applicant stays responsible for the truth of the facts, and the panel says when a draft was written by the AI.
- **Answers in the model's own words.** The facts use the form's labels and option names; the model is told not to copy them, and to put related facts in one sentence (the main reason for the visit and the reason for a holiday visit are one sentence, not two). A payer is described by the relationship and where they live, not "a relative who is my parent". A count is stated as it is given and nothing is added to it. When the applicant's trip description is used, the kind of trip is not repeated.
- **Nothing is zero or empty.** A zero or empty amount or count is not in the facts, and the model is told never to write that something is zero or none unless a fact says so.
- **A fact says only what it says.** For example, where the applicant will be staying is not "booked".
- The tokens `[R1]`... stand for words taken out of the applicant's text: keep each exactly as written where it stood, and never invent one.

**`promptVersion`** (`PROMPT_VERSION` in the prompt file; **3** now; 2 was the prompt of the first polish step; 1 was the prompt of step 8g and was not stored) goes up whenever the prompt wording changes. It is stored on every version that asked the provider (an AI letter, and a template written after an AI letter was rejected) and written to the `cover_letter_generate` audit row (a number, never text). A template written without asking the AI has none, and rows written before it existed stay valid.

**The closing of every letter** is made the same by the server, for AI letters and the template alike (`normalizeClosing` in the checks): the last paragraph, **a blank line, "Yours faithfully," on a line of its own, and the name on the next line**. It copes with the model putting the closing at the end of the last line, writing it twice, using another sign-off ("Kind regards", "Yours sincerely"), signing with a name of its own (that name is dropped: the model never has the real one, and the real name is put in) or leaving the closing out altogether. A closing phrase in the middle of real text is only taken out. A letter that is only a closing is empty. An application with no name ends with a visible `[not provided: applicant's name]` gap instead. A text an administrator writes or edits is **never** changed by this: it is saved as typed.

**The banned-phrase safety net** (`BANNED_PHRASES` in `utils/touristVisaCoverLetterChecks.js`): a short list of patterns an AI letter must not contain. It is **a safety net for the rules above, not a guarantee**: it catches the obvious wording, not every conclusion or boast, so an administrator must still read every letter. Case-insensitive. The ids are `own-words`, `as-expressed`, `details-provided`, `given-on-form`, `the-form`, `information-given`, `compliance`, `meets-requirements`, `immigration-rules`, `enthusiasm`, `looking-forward`, `demonstrating`, `illustrating`, `underscoring`, `ties`, `stability`, `public-funds`, `funds-claims`, `promise`, `return-claim`, `no-intention`, `genuine`, `character`, `confirm`: announcing the applicant's words, remarks about the form or "details provided", claims of compliance or of meeting requirements, immigration rules, enthusiasm, "demonstrating", "illustrating" and "underscoring", ties, stability, funds and public funds, promises and guarantees, return and no-intention claims, "genuine", character claims, and "I confirm". A pattern is ignored for a letter whose applicant's own free text contains it too (they wrote it; the letter may use their words), but only that pattern. Every id has an example in the tests, so a phrase cannot be added without being tested.

**One automatic regeneration.** When an AI letter contains a banned phrase the AI is asked **once more** (the same prompt plus a short reminder that the previous draft was rejected, never quoting it). The second letter goes through every check again. If it is still not acceptable the **template** is used, with the fixed reason `AI_BANNED_PHRASE` (when it fails another check or the provider fails, that reason instead). Other failed checks are **not** asked for again. **The extra request counts towards the daily and the per-administrator limits**: the version records `aiAttempts` (1, or 2), the limits sum it (a row without it counts as 1), and the second request is only made if the limits have room for it after the first (otherwise the template is used at once, with `AI_BANNED_PHRASE`). The banned letters are kept nowhere and logged as a code only.

**The template** (`utils/touristVisaCoverLetterTemplate.js`) is built only from the facts: the same facts give the same letter, a sentence appears only when its fact exists, and a missing essential fact (arrival, departure, reason, nationality, the applicant's name) leaves a visible `[not provided: ...]` gap. **A letter with a gap cannot be approved.** It follows the same rules as the prompt: it states the facts and makes the closing request ("I would be grateful if you would consider my application."), works the applicant's own text in as plain sentences, and says nothing about the form. Tests check it against the banned-phrase list for ten different applications (employed, self-employed, student, retired, someone else paying, earlier travel, a stay at an address, a medical visit, almost nothing, nothing). Its wording is our own and it follows the same tone rules; it says **each answer in its own words from the stored answer, never from the form's option label** (a test checks that no option label of three words or more appears in it), it leaves out a zero or empty fact, and it states an answer no more strongly than the answer does (the audit below). Its structure is fixed, so two letters from it are alike by design: for six different sample applications the 6-word sequences outside the facts that appear in another template letter are 45.9% on average (highest 59.2%).

**The AI adapter** (`backend/services/aiService.js`, with `aiProviders/groq.js` and the settings in `backend/config/ai.js`) is generic: it takes `{ system, user }` and returns `{ text, model, provider }` or throws an error that is one fixed code. **The step for the itinerary reuses it**: add a prompt and a service, not a new adapter.

- Groq's API is OpenAI-compatible: a plain HTTPS `POST https://api.groq.com/openai/v1/chat/completions` with a Bearer key and `fetch`, no SDK. **The address is a constant**; no input of any user reaches it, and a redirect is an error.
- 20-second timeout per request. One retry on a `429` (after the `Retry-After` wait if it is 5 seconds or less, otherwise no retry; 1 second if the header is missing) and one retry on a `5xx`; nothing else is retried. The answer is read with a 256 KB cap.
- **Circuit breaker**: after 3 failed generations in a row the AI is skipped for 5 minutes. It is kept in memory per server process.
- The API key is only ever in the Authorization header. **Nothing from a prompt, an answer, an error body or the key is logged or returned**: the log lines are `AI request failed` with the provider name and a code. Tests capture the logger and the console and look for the key, prompts, bodies and letter text.
- The model: `GROQ_MODEL`, default **`openai/gpt-oss-120b`**, written once, in `backend/config/ai.js`. It was chosen from Groq's model documentation (console.groq.com/docs/models) on 2026-11-03: a **production** (not preview) model, not on a deprecation notice, the flagship open model on Groq, strong at following instructions and at short formal writing. The same page lists `llama-3.3-70b-versatile` and `llama-3.1-8b-instant` as production but the deprecations page gives both a shutdown on 2026-08-16, so they were not used. **Groq changes this list often: before launch, check the current list (`GET https://api.groq.com/openai/v1/models` with your key, or the documentation) and set `GROQ_MODEL` if needed.** The model is a reasoning model: its thinking counts towards the output limit (4000 tokens), which is far more than a 450-word letter needs.
- No real AI call was made while building this: the request follows Groq's OpenAI-compatible documentation and is tested against a fake. The **first real call is the manual test** below.

**The production guard.** Unless `NODE_ENV` is exactly `development` or `test` (step 8j-3: an unset, empty or misspelled `NODE_ENV` counts as production, and so does `Production`), the AI is **not used** (every letter is a template letter, code `AI_TERMS_NOT_CONFIRMED`) unless `AI_DATA_TERMS_CONFIRMED` is exactly `true`. Setting it means someone has (a) confirmed in the Groq console (Data Controls) that **Zero Data Retention** is switched on for the account, and (b) read Groq's terms about whether customer data is used for training. This matters because the letter sends **real trip, employment and money details** (never names, contact details or identifiers) to a third party. Locally, with dummy data, it works without the confirmation.

**Limits.** Per day in total and per administrator per hour, both counted from `touristvisacoverletters` (requests that asked the provider, **including the second request of a regeneration**; template letters that never asked the provider, hand-written letters and skipped requests do not count). Over a limit the request is not refused: it becomes a template letter.

**Data: collection `touristvisacoverletters`** (model `TouristVisaCoverLetter`), one row per **version**, admin only, append only like the audit log (the model refuses every update and delete). Fields: `applicationId`, `version` (1, 2, ...; unique with the application), `text` (**encrypted** with the field cipher, bound to the application and the version: `touristvisacoverletters:<applicationId>:<version>:text`), `source` (`ai`, `template` or `admin`), `model` (the model's name, for `ai`), `fallbackReason` (the fixed code, for a template written when the AI was wanted), `aiAttempted` (whether the provider was asked), `aiAttempts` (1, or 2 after a regeneration; a row without it counts as 1), `promptVersion` (the prompt that was sent; none on a template that never asked), `factsHash`, `basedOnRevision`, `createdBy`, `createdAt`, `status` (`draft` or `approved`), `approvedBy`, `approvedAt`. `factsHash` is an **HMAC-SHA-256** (keyed with the field encryption key) of the facts the version was made from, so a stored hash cannot be used to guess an answer. **The only change ever made to a row is its one-time approval** (`status`, `approvedBy`, `approvedAt`), made by the repository with the database driver and only while the row is still a draft; the model refuses everything else. **On a deployment where automatic index building is switched off, create the indexes once by hand**: `db.touristvisacoverletters.createIndex({ applicationId: 1, version: -1 }, { unique: true })` and `db.touristvisacoverletters.createIndex({ aiAttempted: 1, createdAt: -1 })`.

### 2.7 The AI-assisted itinerary

An administrator can have a **proposed day-by-day itinerary** written for a submitted application, check and edit it, and approve it. It is built in the same style as the cover letter (section 2.6) and reuses everything that is generic there: the AI adapter, the redaction round trip, the production guard, the daily and per-administrator limits, the circuit breaker, the append-only versions, the stale rule and the review-and-approve flow. **Admin only**: the applicant never sees it, there is no applicant page and there is no PDF yet. **Submitting an application never depends on it**: it is written on demand, by a button, by an administrator, and a template or a hand-written itinerary is always possible.

**What it is for, and what it must never be.** It is a *proposed* plan for the visit. It must never invent or imply a booking or any specific: no hotel or restaurant names, no flights, trains, tickets or reservations, no prices, opening times, clock times, phone numbers or links. Only well-known, general sightseeing and activities for the places the applicant named, or for the city they will stay in. The text says so itself (below) and the panel repeats it to the administrator.

**Two roles, split on purpose: the server writes the skeleton, the AI writes the words under each heading.**

- **The skeleton** (`backend/utils/touristVisaItinerarySkeleton.js`, pure) is built by the server from the arrival and departure dates. For a stay of **up to 21 nights** there is one heading for every day, the arrival day and the departure day included: `Day 1, Monday 15 March 2027`. The number of headings is the number of nights plus one, so 15 March to 2 April 2027 (18 nights) is **19 headings**. For a stay of **more than 21 nights** there are **weekly blocks**, headed by their date range (`Monday 15 March 2027 to Sunday 21 March 2027`; the last block may be shorter, and a block of one day is headed by that day). Dates are worked out in UTC arithmetic, so no time zone and no change of clocks moves a date or a weekday. A visit of more than 183 nights (six months) is refused as invalid, which also keeps the whole text inside the 8000 characters an administrator may save. **The AI never writes a heading, a date or a weekday.**
- **The AI** is asked for **JSON only**: `{"descriptions": ["...", ...]}` with exactly one short description for each heading, in order. The prompt lists the numbered headings (marking the arrival and the departure day) and the facts.

**Missing or impossible dates.** If the arrival (1.6) or the departure (1.7) is missing, or the departure is before the arrival, or the visit is longer than six months, generating is refused with a **422** and a fixed code, `ITINERARY_DATES_MISSING` or `ITINERARY_DATES_INVALID`, plus `questions` (the numbers of the questions to fix, `["1.6", "1.7"]`). The view carries the same thing as `blocker`, so the panel tells the administrator which answers to fix (with the Edit button of section 1) and switches Generate off. The API still accepts a hand-written version and an approval; the panel does not offer a blank text box while no version exists, so the dates are fixed first (or a template is made once they are). Nothing is written, and the refusal is not audited as a generation.

**The privacy design: its own, smaller allowlist.** `shared/tourist-visa/itineraryFacts.js` (copied to the client and the backend by `npm run sync:shared`, with its `.d.ts`) picks the specs it needs from the cover letter's allowlist (the same question, read the same way) and is checked by the **same code** (`validateAllowlist` and `buildFactsFor` in `coverLetterFacts.js`, which now take their rules as an argument; the cover letter's own rules and behaviour are unchanged and its tests still pass) against its **own rules** (`ITINERARY_RULES`): only sections `1` and `6C` may be read (`allowedSections`); every other section is refused (`forbiddenSections`); the question ids listed below are refused even inside those two sections (`excludedIds`); a question of type amount, number, email, telephone, file or declaration is refused (`forbiddenTypes`); an address can only be read as its town; and a placeholder for a name or an employer is refused outright (`noTokenSpecs`). `buildItineraryFacts` runs the validation every time and **refuses to build** (`FACTS_UNSAFE`, then a 500 `ITINERARY_UNAVAILABLE`) if it finds a problem.

**Every question id on the itinerary allowlist (10 questions):**

| Fact | Question id | How it is used |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Planned arrival date | `q1_6` | the date in words; also the start of the skeleton |
| Planned departure date | `q1_7` | the date in words; also the end of the skeleton |
| Length of the visit | `q1_6`, `q1_7` | worked out: nights between the two dates |
| Main reason for the visit | `q1_1` | the answer in words; left out when 1.1 is Medical |
| Reason for the holiday visit | `q1_4` | the answer in words; left out when 1.1 is Medical |
| Travelling as part of an organised group | `q1_2` | the answer in words (Yes or No) |
| Number of travelling companions | `q1_3` | only how many companions entries there are |
| The places and plans for the trip | `q1_5` | free text, **redacted exactly like the cover letter's** (tokens for names, emails, telephone numbers, links and anything in square brackets, cut to 500 characters, passed in a `<data>` block); left out when 1.1 is Medical |
| Plans for accommodation (the type of stay) | `q6_7x` | said in words that cannot suggest a booking: "a paid stay", "with family or friends", "not decided yet", "somewhere not yet known", "somewhere else" (the form's own option says "I will book a hotel...") |
| Area or city they expect to stay in | `q6_7y` | **only the well-known places it names**, from the fixed list in `places.js`; never the text typed; nothing at all when none is named (the plan then says "the United Kingdom") |
| Town or city of the address where they will stay | `q6_7b` | only the town of the address; a town with a digit, a comma or over 60 characters is dropped; the rest is scrubbed |

Plus **one derived fact**, "Places the applicant named, in order", worked out from the trip description (1.5) against a **fixed list of about 100 well-known UK places** (`PLACES` in `shared/tourist-visa/places.js`, shared with the cover letter: cities, towns, regions and a few landmarks). Only a name from our list is ever returned, so nothing the applicant typed travels by this fact. A name that is also an ordinary word (Bath, Derby, Hull, Perth, Kew, Wales, Durham, Windsor, Chester, Lancaster, Highlands) counts only with a capital letter ("have a bath" is not a place). The template uses the order of the places, and the checks use it to see that an itinerary names only these places (or the city of the stay) and keeps their order.

| Telephone | section `2D` |

**Tests prove it**: the exact list of the 10 ids is asserted (so widening it fails a test); every other one of the config's 201 questions is tried against the validator, and **adding any one of them is refused**; every id of every excluded section is checked not to be on the list; a made-up rich application (money, employer, parents, dependants, passport, Part 9, a stranger's contact details) is built and none of its secrets reaches the facts. The facts have no line for the applicant's name: the AI is not asked for it.

**Validation of what the AI returns** (`backend/utils/touristVisaItineraryChecks.js`, pure). A failed check returns a **fixed code** and the number of the entry and the id of the rule, never any text.

| Code | The answer... |
| --- | --- |
| `ITINERARY_BAD_JSON` | is not JSON (a code fence is tolerated), is not an object, has a key other than `descriptions`, or `descriptions` is not an array |
| `ITINERARY_WRONG_COUNT` | has more or fewer entries than there are headings |
| `ITINERARY_BAD_ENTRY` | has an entry that is not a string, not plain (a line break, a bullet, a number, markdown), shorter than 20 characters, or longer than 300 (a day) or 240 (a week); **or a last description that does not say the traveller leaves the United Kingdom or goes home** (leaves, departs, returns, home, sets off, heads back); **or a description that repeats an earlier one**: the same sentence, the same run of five words, or the same first three words (rules `repeat-sentence`, `repeat-sequence`, `same-opening`; case, punctuation and apostrophes do not hide a repeat) |
| `ITINERARY_FORBIDDEN_CONTENT` | has an entry with a **digit** (so any date, price, time, phone number or token like `[R1]`), a currency symbol or word, a clock-time word (noon, midday, o'clock), a link, an email, a phone word, a bracket, or a weekday or month name (the headings carry the dates, and a description must never contradict them) |
| `ITINERARY_BANNED_PHRASE` | has an entry with booked, reserved, confirmed, guaranteed, tickets, admission, flights, trains, airlines, hotels, hostels, prices, costs, fees, discounts, budget, opening hours, marketing words (breathtaking, must-see, unforgettable, stunning, iconic, world-famous, hidden gems, magical, amazing...), scene-setting words (absorbing, soaking, immersing, picturesque, charming, majestic, captivating, and the other forms of those verbs), **"next destination" or "onward"** (the last day is a departure), or any of the cover letter's own banned patterns |
| `ITINERARY_PLACE_NOT_NAMED` | names a well-known place that is neither one the applicant named nor the city of the stay (a country is fine) |
| `ITINERARY_PLACE_ORDER` | names the places in a different order from the applicant's |

The banned list is, like the cover letter's, **a safety net, not a guarantee**: it catches the obvious wording. The place check only knows the places on our list, so an AI that names a town that is not on it is not caught by code; the prompt forbids it and the administrator reads every itinerary.

**The last day and the plain wording (prompt version 2, from the first real test).** The AI had written things like "absorbing the city's varied architecture" and "soaking in the cultural atmosphere", and a last day that could read as onward travel. Version 2 of the prompt (and of the user message) says so explicitly:

- **The last heading is a departure from the United Kingdom**: it says that the traveller leaves the United Kingdom or sets off on the journey home, in the model's own words, and it never mentions a next destination, onward travel or any place outside the United Kingdom. The template's last day already read "set off on the journey home" and is unchanged. The user message repeats it after the list of headings, and the retry reminder asks for it.
- **Plain, factual wording only**: one or two short sentences per day saying what the traveller will do and where; no scene-setting, no feelings, no descriptions of atmosphere, architecture, culture or beauty, no sales language; the words "absorbing", "soaking in", "immersing", "picturesque", "charming", "majestic" and "captivating" are named as wording not to write. There is still no sample itinerary or sample sentence in the prompt (a test checks that the phrase "journey home" appears once, as guidance, and that there is no example).
- **The checks back it up** (the same fixed codes, so a draft that fails is asked for once more and then falls back to the template): the banned list now has `scene-setting` (absorb..., soak..., immers..., picturesque, charming, majestic, captivating, breathtaking, stunning, unforgettable, must-see, iconic) and `onward-travel` ("next destination", "onward", "onwards"), both `ITINERARY_BANNED_PHRASE`; and the **last description must say that the traveller leaves, departs, returns, goes home, sets off or heads back** (`ITINERARY_BAD_ENTRY`, rule `last-day`, asked of every AI answer by the service and of the template in its tests). A test checks that the template uses none of the banned words, for every stay length, and always ends with a departure that says "home".

**Specific but safe days, variety and British spelling (prompt version 3, from the second real test).** The first plain version was thin and repetitive ("Extend the Bath itinerary with more sightseeing", "Add more Edinburgh sightseeing to the schedule", "Continue a relaxed day in ..."). Version 3 of the prompt asks for more, and the checks make the worst of the repetition impossible to keep:

- **Specific but safe.** Each ordinary day describes one or two specific, well-known sights or activities in that day's city, **naming real landmarks** (the prompt names the kinds, a museum, castle, abbey, cathedral, park, market, historic street, and **no landmark itself**, a test checks this), in plain factual wording. **A landmark is named only if the model is certain it exists in that city; if unsure it describes the kind of place.** Nothing is booked, ticketed, timed or priced (the earlier rules stand). A day in an unknown or unlisted place stays generic, as before.
- **Variety.** No landmark on more than one day; no two days with the same sentence or the same opening words; varied verbs; at most one or two quieter days in the whole stay, and a quieter day still says what the traveller does; a travel day says the route (from where to where) and one short activity on arrival, and does not say how the traveller travels. British English throughout. The arrival and the last day keep their own rules.
- **The repetition checks** (`repeatedIn` in `touristVisaItineraryChecks.js`, asked for by the service for every AI answer, with the existing fixed code `ITINERARY_BAD_ENTRY` and the retry once then the template): two descriptions that share a **sentence**, a **run of five words**, or their **first three words** are refused (case, punctuation and apostrophes do not hide a repeat; a repeat inside one description is not looked for). They apply to the AI's answers only: the template is deterministic and reuses its own sentences by design, and an administrator's edit is never checked.
- **British spelling.** Common American spellings are changed to British ones in **every AI itinerary and in the template text**, by `britishSpelling`, from **one constant**, `AMERICAN_TO_BRITISH` (about 100 forms: the -ize verbs with every form, finalize, organize, recognize, realize, emphasize, summarize, specialize, apologize, criticize, memorize, customize, popularize, visualize; organization; center, theater, favorite, color, traveling and their forms; harbor, neighbor, neighborhood). It is **whole-word only** (so Colorado, recolor, centerpiece, theatergoer, organic, organism and the like are never touched), **case-aware** (center, Center and CENTER become centre, Centre and CENTRE) and runs **before the other checks**, so the checks and the stored text both see the British form. A name that is spelled the American way in the United Kingdom is kept (`KEEP_AS_WRITTEN`: Center Parcs). A test with tricky words covers it. An administrator's own edit is saved as typed.
- **These are safety nets, not guarantees, and admin review stays mandatory.** The code cannot check that a landmark exists or is in the right city (the place check only knows the UK places on the fixed list, and a landmark is not one), so an invented sight is not caught: that is why the prompt asks for certainty and for the kind of place when unsure, and why the administrator reads every itinerary. The repetition and spelling checks are word checks and miss what they do not list.

**The final text.** The title line `Proposed itinerary`, one neutral sentence, `This is a proposed plan for the visit, and it may change.`, then each heading with its description under it, separated by blank lines. A text an administrator writes or edits is saved as typed and never changed.

**Layers of fallback**, in the order a request meets them. A fixed code is recorded, never any text.

1. The AI is off (`AI_DISABLED`), not set up (`AI_NOT_CONFIGURED`), or in production without the data-terms confirmation (`AI_TERMS_NOT_CONFIRMED`) -> template, no request.
2. The circuit breaker is open (`AI_CIRCUIT_OPEN`), the daily limit (`AI_DAILY_LIMIT`) or the administrator's hourly limit (`AI_ADMIN_LIMIT`) is reached -> template, no request.
3. The provider is asked and fails (`AI_RATE_LIMITED`, `AI_TIMEOUT`, `AI_NETWORK`, `AI_PROVIDER_REJECTED`, `AI_PROVIDER_ERROR`, `AI_BAD_RESPONSE`, `AI_TRUNCATED`) -> template at once.
4. The provider answers but the answer fails any check above -> **one automatic second request**, with a short reminder that the previous answer was rejected (it never quotes it). If the second answer fails too, or the limits have no room for the extra request, -> template with the code of the failure.

The template fallback is a normal `201`, not an error, and the administrator is shown a plain sentence for each reason. **The extra request counts towards the limits**: the version records `aiAttempts` (1 or 2), exactly as for the cover letter. Because the answer to the stay of 18 nights needs about 19 short entries, the output limit of the adapter (4000 tokens, shared) is enough for a daily itinerary; a six-month stay in weekly blocks may be cut off (`AI_TRUNCATED`) and then gets the template.

**The template** (`backend/utils/touristVisaItineraryTemplate.js`, pure and deterministic) writes the description under each heading from the facts alone, in plain factual wording of our own: an **arrival day** (arrive in the first place named, or in the city of the stay, or "in the United Kingdom"); the **middle days spread across the places named, in the order named** (the earlier places take the extra days; the first day in a new place is a travel-and-settle day; the last day in a place says that the next day moves on; every sixth day is a day of rest), or the city of the stay when no place was named; and a **departure day**. The pool of activities depends on the purpose (tourism, visiting family or friends, other). A long stay is described a week at a time with the same rules. It states no booking, price, time or hotel, and **a test runs its output through the same checks as the AI's**, for every length from one day to six months, daily and weekly.

**The prompt** is in one file, `backend/config/touristVisaItineraryPrompt.js`, **version 3** (`ITINERARY_PROMPT_VERSION`; goes up whenever the wording changes; stored on every version that asked the provider and written to the `itinerary_generate` audit row; 1 was the first prompt, 2 added the last-day and plain-wording rules). JSON only; one or two short, plain sentences on one line for each heading, saying what the traveller will do; one or two specific, well-known sights or activities in that day's city for the places named, in the order named, or the city of the stay, naming a landmark only when the model is certain it exists there, generic where no place is known; an arrival day, a departure day, and travel or rest days where the facts suggest; never name an unnamed place; nothing that invents or implies a booking or a specific; no digits, dates, weekdays, months, currency, brackets or tokens; no promises or marketing; do not copy the applicant's words; tone formal and plain, in British English, varied sentences and openings, no filler. **There is no sample itinerary, model day or stock phrasing in the prompt** (a test checks this) and nothing in the prompt or the code is meant to hide that an AI helped: the panel says when a draft was written by the AI.

**Data: collection `touristvisaitineraries`** (model `TouristVisaItinerary`), separate from the cover letters, the same version model: one row per **version**, admin only, append only (the model refuses every update and delete). Fields: `applicationId`, `version` (unique with the application), `text` (**encrypted** with the field cipher, bound to the application and the version: `touristvisaitineraries:<applicationId>:<version>:text`), `source` (`ai`, `template` or `admin`), `model`, `fallbackReason` (the fixed code), `aiAttempted`, `aiAttempts` (1 or 2), `promptVersion`, `factsHash` (an **HMAC-SHA-256** with the field encryption key, over the itinerary's own facts, with the prefix `touristvisaitinerary:facts:v1:`), `basedOnRevision`, `createdBy`, `createdAt`, `status` (`draft` or `approved`), `approvedBy`, `approvedAt`. The only change ever made to a row is its one-time approval, made by the repository with the database driver and only while the row is still a draft. **On a deployment where automatic index building is switched off, create the indexes once by hand**: `db.touristvisaitineraries.createIndex({ applicationId: 1, version: -1 }, { unique: true })` and `db.touristvisaitineraries.createIndex({ aiAttempted: 1, createdAt: -1 })`.

**Shared limits.** The daily and per-administrator limits are **shared** with the cover letter: one allowance for the AI. A request for an itinerary uses the same allowance as one for a cover letter, in both directions. The production guard and the circuit breaker are the adapter's, and shared too.

### 2.8 PDFs: the foundation, the application summary, the cover letter and the itinerary (steps 8h-1 to 8h-3)

**What it is.** A signed-in user can download **the application summary**, a PDF of every answer in the layout of an official printout: a header block, then every part and section in order as rows of question and answer. It is built on a **reusable foundation** (`backend/utils/pdf/pdfBuilder.js`) that the cover letter PDF (step 8h-2, further down) is built on, and the itinerary PDF (step 8h-3) will use.

**Branding rules, and why we do not copy the government identity.** The layout follows an official printout (a header with a reference, a block of uppercase labels and values, sections of question and answer rows, a footer with "Page X of Y"), **but the file must not look official**, because a client could pass it off as a UK Government document or an applicant could think it is one. So it never carries a crest, the words "UK Visas & Immigration", a barcode, a GWF-style number or the heading "Partially completed application". It carries the Wiser Consulting logo (a copy of `client/public/logo.png` in `backend/assets/pdf/wiser-logo.png`, because the backend is deployed on its own, **with the white background made transparent in step 8h-2** (the original is 697 by 566 pixels, about 926 dpi at the size it is printed, and is never scaled up; the white that touched the border was an opaque box, now gone, and the white inside the globe is kept). **If the site's logo changes, copy it again and make its background transparent**), the site's navy and green (`PALETTE` in `backend/config/touristVisaPdf.js`), the title **"Application summary"**, the application reference as **plain text** (never a barcode), and on **every page** the footer line "Prepared by Wiser Consulting from the applicant's answers. This is not an official UK Government document." Tests check the footer on every page and that none of the forbidden words or marks is in any file. The example printout the layout was modelled on, and any real data, are **not in the repository**.

**Layout.** A4 portrait, margins of 19 mm (the rule is 18 mm at least; a test checks nothing is drawn outside them), light tints only and no large dark fills, so it prints well and reads in black and white. Every page: the logo on the left and the reference on the right as text, a thin rule; at the foot a thin rule, the applicant's name and reference on the left, the disclaimer line, and "Page X of Y" on the right. Page 1: the title, then the header block (APPLICANT NAME, APPLICATION REFERENCE, PASSPORT NUMBER, COUNTRY OF NATIONALITY, DATE OF BIRTH, GENDER, DATE OF APPLICATION (the submission date), TYPE OF VISA ("UK Standard Visitor"), SUBMISSION METHOD ("Online, Wiser Consulting"), CONTACT EMAIL, CONTACT LANGUAGE; a missing value prints a dash; **no fee, service option or payment reference**), then every part and section from the shared review builder (`buildReview`) over **the config snapshot the application was submitted under** (so it shows what the applicant and the Overview show). Each section has a bold heading with a thin rule above and below; each row has the question on the left (about 45 percent) and the answer on the right with a hairline between rows and a heavier rule between groups of questions; Yes and No are bold; addresses and long text wrap with their line breaks kept; repeating-group entries are numbered under a tinted title; files are listed by **name and size only** (no link, no storage id); a row an admin corrected carries "Corrected by our team on <date>" (**no old value and no reason**). **A row is never split across pages** (it moves to the next page whole), **a heading is never alone at the foot of a page** (it keeps with its first row; a part keeps with its first section and row), and an answer taller than a page continues on the next ("(continued)"). **Rows stay with the rows they belong to (step 8h-2):** a follow-up row (the "Please give details" kind: the same question number as the row before it) stays on the page of the question it follows; the last two rows of a section or of an entry stay together, so a page never starts with one row that has lost its company; a repeating entry that fits on a page is **never split** (its title and every row are on one page), and one that is too tall keeps its title with at least its first two rows; the row that counts the entries stays with the first entry; and a section heading keeps with at least its first two rows. The price is some white space at the foot of a page, which is intended. The section number (such as 10D) is set in the bold sans and the title in the serif, because the serif's 1 and 0 read like I and O at heading size. **Notes, flags, the change history, reasons and audit rows are never read or printed.**

**Fonts** (bundled in `backend/assets/fonts/`, about 3.6 MB with the logo; **all SIL Open Font License 1.1, each with its licence text next to it**): **Playfair Display** (Regular and Bold; titles and headings; the lining-figures feature is switched on because its default figures are old-style), **Source Sans 3** (Regular, Bold and Italic; questions, answers, header and footer) and **Source Serif 4** (Regular, Bold, Italic and Bold Italic; the body of the letter and the descriptions in the itinerary, **not used by the application summary**) and, **new in step 8h-3, Cormorant Garamond** (SemiBold and SemiBold Italic, 1.07 MB in all; the display face of the letter's name and the itinerary's title, and the italic of the day titles; its lining figures are switched on). No script or handwriting font is used. The files came from the npm packages `@expo-google-fonts/playfair-display`, `@expo-google-fonts/source-sans-3`, `@expo-google-fonts/source-serif-4` and `@expo-google-fonts/cormorant-garamond` (versions 0.4.2, 0.4.2 and 0.4.1; the packages are "MIT AND OFL-1.1", the fonts are OFL-1.1) and the upstream projects are Adobe's Source Sans and Source Serif (`github.com/adobe-fonts`), the Playfair Display project (`github.com/clauseggers/Playfair-Display`) and the Cormorant project (`github.com/CatharsisFonts/Cormorant`); the licence texts are `OFL-SourceSans3.txt`, `OFL-SourceSerif4.txt`, `OFL-PlayfairDisplay.txt` and `OFL-CormorantGaramond.txt` (the Cormorant files are the SemiBold and the SemiBold Italic of that package, and cover everything printed: Latin with accents, the pound sign, figures). The fonts are **embedded** (pdfkit is told `font: null`, so no standard font is referred to) and only the glyphs used are included.

**The palette** (step 8h-3), defined once as the named constants `BRAND` in `backend/config/touristVisaPdf.js` and used by every PDF (the older names the application summary uses, `PALETTE`, are made from it): the **green of the Wiser logo** (`#4a634a`, sampled from the logo), a deep green-black **ink** (`#16201a`) for text, one warm **terracotta** accent (`#9a5632`), their deep forms for titles, a light **tint** of the green and of the terracotta for the day blocks, and the greys of the rules. **Print contrast** (a test checks each): white on the green 6.6:1 and on the terracotta 5.6:1, ink on either tint above 14:1, the muted grey 6.5:1, the deep titles above 7:1. **Greyscale:** both tints are about 94 percent light, so the day blocks are quiet on a greyscale printer; both bands are deep greys, and the day label, not the colour, carries the meaning. Never a full-page fill: the colour is the bands, the thin edge of the letter and small rules, so the files cost little ink.

**Latin text only.** Everything printed is normalised first (`backend/utils/pdf/pdfText.js`): composed accents, every kind of hyphen (including the non-breaking hyphen, U+2011, that prints as a box) becomes a normal hyphen, every kind of space one space, and invisible and control characters are dropped; since step 8h-2 the typographic characters become **the closest plain ones before the Latin-only check**: curly and low single quotes and the prime become a straight apostrophe, curly double quotes a straight double quote, the en dash, the em dash and the horizontal bar a plain hyphen, and the ellipsis character three full stops (so a name typed with a curly apostrophe prints with a straight one, and a sentence with an em dash reads with a hyphen); accented Latin letters, the pound sign, the bullet and the middle dot print as typed (a test for each of them and for each plain-ing). **If any text has a character the fonts cannot print (Arabic, Chinese, Thai, Hebrew, Devanagari, most emoji), the whole PDF is refused** with **422 `PDF_UNSUPPORTED_CHARACTERS`** and a plain message that names the section ("The text in section 1 (Travel information) has characters that the PDF cannot print, because it prints Latin letters only. Correct that answer and try again."; "The application details at the top of the summary ..." for the name or email in the header block). **The character itself is never in the message or the log**, and nothing is recorded for a refused PDF. A blank or a box on paper is never an option. (Source Sans 3 also covers Cyrillic and Greek, so those print; an administrator can correct any answer through the normal correction route.)

**Routes** (behind the feature switch and `isAuthorized`; in memory only: never written to disk, Cloudinary or the database; each answer is `Content-Type: application/pdf`, `Content-Disposition: attachment` with a file name built **only from the reference** such as `TV-X42GYXPS-application-summary.pdf` (never a name), `Cache-Control: no-store` and `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`):

| Route | Who | What |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `GET /api/tourist-visa/admin/applications/:id/pdf/answers` | an administrator (`isAdmin`) | the PDF of any submitted, in-review or completed application. A draft or an unknown id is the same 404 as everywhere. |
| `GET /api/tourist-visa/admin/applications/:id/pdf/cover-letter` | an administrator (`isAdmin`) | the **newest version of the cover letter** as a PDF (step 8h-2; no applicant route). File name `TV-XXXXXXXX-cover-letter.pdf`, or `TV-XXXXXXXX-cover-letter-draft.pdf` when that version is not approved. No letter: 404 `LETTER_NOT_FOUND`; a draft application or unknown id: the same 404 as everywhere. Any status after submit (a completed application can export a letter that exists). |
| `GET /api/tourist-visa/admin/applications/:id/pdf/itinerary` | an administrator (`isAdmin`) | the **newest version of the itinerary** as a PDF (step 8h-3; no applicant route). File name `TV-XXXXXXXX-itinerary.pdf`, or `TV-XXXXXXXX-itinerary-draft.pdf` when that version is not approved. No itinerary: 404 `ITINERARY_NOT_FOUND`; unreadable: 500 `ITINERARY_UNREADABLE`; a draft application or unknown id: the same 404 as everywhere. Any status after submit (a completed application can export an itinerary that exists). |

Anonymous requests get 401 and a non-administrator on the admin route 403. Both routes share **one rate limit per signed-in user** (`backend/middleware/touristVisaPdfLimit.js`: 20 PDFs in 10 minutes, then 429 `TOO_MANY_PDFS`; it counts in the server process's memory, like the upload limit), applied before anything is decrypted or drawn. A runaway document is stopped at 150 pages (422 `PDF_TOO_LARGE`); any other failure is 500 `PDF_FAILED` with a fixed message and a log line holding only the application's id.

**Privacy and audit.** Decrypted values are only ever in the generated file, which is built in memory and sent. The **PDF metadata** (title, author, subject, keywords, creator, producer) holds fixed words only, no personal data, and the file holds no script, action or link (tests check the bytes). Every successful export is written to the audit log **before the file is made** as **`pdf_export`** with `pdfType` (`answers` or `cover_letter`; the itinerary will add `itinerary`) and `pdfBy` (`admin` or `applicant`), and for a cover letter also `version` and `pdfApproved` (a yes or no): ids and fixed words only, never any content, a name, the reference or a file name. **`adminId` holds the id of whoever downloaded it, so for an applicant's own copy it is the applicant's user id**; the Activity panel words it as "exported the application summary (PDF)" or "downloaded a copy of their application (PDF)". Nothing sensitive reaches a log line or an error.

**The page.** On the Overview (`/admin/tourist-visa/<id>`), a button **"Export application (PDF)"** in the header area beside the status (`PdfDownloadButton.tsx`, shared with the applicant's page); on the applicant's confirmation page after a submit (`/tourist-visa/review`), **"Download a copy (PDF)"** in the card's footer. The page fetches the file with the session's token (a plain link could not carry it), holds it in memory as a blob and saves it under `<reference>-application-summary.pdf`; nothing is kept. The button shows a busy state (a spinner, "Making the PDF…", `aria-busy` and `aria-disabled`, so a second click does nothing and focus is not lost), announces to screen readers, and **every failure shows the site's own toast** with plain words (the unsupported-characters sentence is the server's own, which names the section and never repeats an answer; a 401 signs the user out). 375 px and up, light and dark, tap target at least 40 px, no native dialog.

**The cover letter PDF (step 8h-2).** `GET /admin/applications/:id/pdf/cover-letter` (administrators only, the same feature gate, `isAuthorized`, `isAdmin`, limiter, no-store headers, in-memory handling and audit pattern as the answers PDF). Built from `backend/utils/touristVisaCoverLetterPdf.js` (the layout and the parsing) and `backend/utils/pdf/letterBuilder.js` (the letter's page, on `documentBuilder.js`, the designed-document page the itinerary shares), through `coverLetterForAdmin` in `touristVisaPdfService.js`. No AI prompt, AI service or stored data was changed: it only reads, with one new read method, `findLatestWithText`, on the cover letter repository.

- **The text is never changed.** The stored text is laid out as it is: the salutation, the paragraphs (separated by a blank line; the line breaks inside one are kept) and the closing ("Yours faithfully," and the name under it, or "Kind regards", or a name in a block of its own). Nothing is added, removed or reworded; only the typographic characters are made plain (the normalisation above) and runs of spaces become one. A test puts the printed body back together and compares it with the stored text. Only layout furniture is added: the sender block, the date line, the reference line, and the room for a signature.
- **Which version.** The **newest** version. If it is approved, the letter is clean. **If it is not approved, the export is still allowed** but every page carries a large diagonal watermark and a small banner at the top, both reading "DRAFT - NOT APPROVED" (text in the file, so it can be searched), and the file name ends "-draft". An approved letter has neither, anywhere. A newer draft written after an approval is therefore a draft again, even though an older version is approved. A stale approved letter (the answers changed after it) is **not** marked: the PDF does not check staleness.
- **The letterhead** (step 8h-3) is the applicant's **name** (given and family names, 2.1 and 2.2), large at the top left in the display face, a short terracotta rule under it, and one quiet row of small type with thin separators: the **home address** (2.24: line 1, line 2, town and region, postcode, country), the **email** (2.4) and the **telephone** (2.32), **only what the answers hold** (a long row flows onto a second row). Under it a hairline, then one tidy line: **the date** on the left and the **reference** on the right. **The date** is **the day the version was written** (the stored `createdAt`), and failing that the day it was approved; the stored text holds no date of its own. (If the senior wants the approval date on an approved letter instead, change `letterDate` in `touristVisaCoverLetterPdf.js`: it is one function with its own tests.)
- **Design.** A4, margins of 25 mm. The body in Source Serif 4 at 11 pt on an 18 pt line in a **column of about 70 characters** (410 pt, with 12 pt between paragraphs; it does not run to the right margin). **A drop cap was considered and left out**: the stored text always opens with its salutation ("Dear Sir or Madam,"), which a drop cap would only break. **A narrow brand edge** runs down the left of every page (a thin green line with a short terracotta start, 3.2 pt wide: under one percent of the page). No logo and nothing that looks official (the letter is the applicant's own). The closing has **a real signature line** (a thin line 176 pt long in the room left for the signature) with the printed name below it. A slim footer on every page: the reference, "Prepared by Wiser Consulting", and **"Page X of Y" always**. A longer letter flows to further pages with a **compact continuation header** (the name in the display face and a rule with a terracotta tick), not the letterhead.
- **Page behaviour.** A short letter is one page. A paragraph is not split to leave one line at the foot or head of a page (four lines or more may be split, leaving at least two on each side). **The closing is never alone on a page**: it is placed as one group with at least **the last two lines of the body**, the signature space (with its line) and the name, and the group moves to a new page whole if it does not fit. A very long word with no space is broken to fit inside the margins.
- **Fit to one page** (step 8h-4). A letter that overflows page 1 by a few lines would put only its last paragraph and the closing on page 2, which looks unfinished. So the letter is tried at the steps of `FIT_STEPS` in order, from the nominal setting to the tightest, and **the first step at which the whole letter (the closing and the signature room included) fits on one page is used: the least tightening that works**. The order: **(1) the line and paragraph spacing** (the line 18 pt to 17 pt, the paragraph gap 12 pt to 9 pt, in four steps), **(2) the space round the letterhead** (its gaps scaled to 80, 60 and 40 percent), **(3) the size of the body by at most half a point** (11 to 10.75 to 10.5 pt, on a line of 16.7 and 16.4 pt). **The minimums:** the body never under 10.5 pt, the line never under 1.5 times the size, the signature room (46 pt, with its line) never tightened. **A letter that already fits is not touched** (step 0 is the nominal setting, drawn exactly as before), and **a letter that does not fit even at the tightest step flows to two pages exactly as before: at the nominal setting**, the closing kept with at least the last two body lines, a compact continuation header. The draft banner and watermark and everything else are as they were. How much it takes: with the template letter an overflow of 3 lines uses step 2 (a 17.3 pt line, 10 pt between paragraphs) for an approved letter and step 4 for a draft (its banner takes room); a letter that overflows by up to about 7 lines (about 6 for a draft) still fits on one page, at the tightest step. A letter is tried on a probe page that is thrown away (opening the fonts is the slow part, so one is shared by the attempts) and the wrapped text is worked out once for each size. `fitOf(model)` returns the step, `fitsAtStep` tests one, for the tests.
- **Refusal.** Text the fonts cannot print, in the letter or in the letterhead (the name, address, email or telephone), is refused before anything is recorded or drawn: **422 `PDF_UNSUPPORTED_CHARACTERS`** with `section` `letter` ("The text of the cover letter has characters that the PDF cannot print... Edit the letter, save it as a new version and try again.") or `sender` ("The applicant's name or address, printed at the top of the letter, has characters that ... Correct that answer and try again."). The characters are never in the message or a log. A letter that cannot be decrypted is a fixed 500 `LETTER_UNREADABLE` with nothing recorded.
- **Audit.** One `pdf_export` row with `pdfType: "cover_letter"`, `pdfBy: "admin"`, `version` (the printed version) and `pdfApproved` (a yes or no); ids and fixed facts only, **never any letter text**. The Activity panel words it as "downloaded version 2 of the cover letter as a PDF (approved)" or "(a draft, not approved)". The metadata is fixed words only (title "Cover letter"; no personal data) and the file holds no script.
- **The button.** In the Cover letter panel on the Overview, **"Download PDF"** (`PdfDownloadButton` with `kind="cover_letter"`): off, with the hint "Write a cover letter first...", until a letter exists; on for a draft (the hint says the PDF will be marked as a draft) and for an approved version; off while the panel is saving or generating; and when the text box holds unsaved changes the hint says the PDF holds **the saved version**, not the changes. The same download pattern, busy state and toasts as the answers PDF, with plain words for a 404 (no letter), a 422 (the server's sentence), a 429 and any other failure.

**The itinerary PDF (step 8h-3).** `GET /admin/applications/:id/pdf/itinerary`: administrators only, with the cover letter's gate, limiter (the one shared budget), no-store headers, in-memory handling, refusal and audit pattern, through `itineraryForAdmin` in `touristVisaPdfService.js`. Built from `backend/utils/touristVisaItineraryPdf.js` on the same `documentBuilder.js` page. No AI prompt, AI service or stored data was changed: one new read method, `findLatestWithText`, on the itinerary repository.

- **What is stored** (read first, as the task asked). An itinerary is **plain text**, made by `assembleItinerary` in `touristVisaItineraryChecks.js`: a **title line** ("Proposed itinerary"), **one neutral note** ("This is a proposed plan for the visit, and it may change."), then **one block for each day**, each a **heading line and one description line** (one or two short sentences), the blocks separated by a blank line. The heading is **"Day 1, Monday 15 March 2027"** for a stay of up to 21 nights (a block for every day, the arrival and the departure day included), and for a longer stay a **week at a time**: "Monday 15 March 2027 to Sunday 21 March 2027" (a block of one day is just its date). **There are no clock times, no places other than the ones inside the sentences, no bullets and no labels beyond these**, and at most 8000 characters. So the PDF has **no time column** unless an administrator's own edit starts a line with a clock time ("09:30 Breakfast", "9am", "2.15 pm"), in which case that line gets a time in a column of its own (and only then); nothing is invented.
- **What is printed, as stored.** Every sentence is printed as it is stored. The stored **title line is the small kicker** above the large "Travel itinerary" (the furniture title) and the stored **note** is printed under it; the heading of a day is split between its **band** ("DAY 1" turned on its side in white, and the short date beside it) and the **title** next to the band (the rest of the heading, in the italic display face); the **description** is the row. A test puts the printed words back together and compares them with the stored text plus the furniture, for 1, 3, 5, 14 and 30 nights: nothing added, nothing removed. A hand-edited itinerary is read too (no title, a paragraph that is not a heading, a heading with no text, a heading typed with no blank line before it).
- **The details grid** under the title is taken from the **answers** (the facts the itinerary is built on), **only the fields the data has**: the traveller, the destination (the places the trip description names, from the fixed list of places; none named, no destination is invented), the arrival and the departure date, the length of stay (not for a day trip) and where the traveller stays (a plan, or the towns named). A field the answers do not hold is not there.
- **The layout** (A4, 20 mm margins). Page 1: the kicker, the large title (Cormorant Garamond SemiBold), a short terracotta rule, the note in italic, the grid in a soft green panel. Then **one block for each day, stacked**: a rounded block with a soft tint, a **band on the left** (alternately the brand green and the terracotta, so the pages have rhythm) with the day label on its side and the date beside it, the day's **title in italic** and the description. **A block is never split across pages** (one row so long that a block would be taller than a page is the one exception: it carries on in a second block, band and title repeated). From the second page on: a compact header (the title and the traveller's name) and the slim footer (reference, "Prepared by Wiser Consulting", "Page X of Y").
- **The type tiers and the page-balancing rule** (step 8h-4 replaced the fill rule of 8h-3). The type is set by the number of days, so that a short trip is large and a long one compact and the type never grows as a trip gets longer: **one or two days "grand"** (a wide band, a 34 pt title), **three to nine "comfortable"**, **ten or more "compact"** (the tiers are unchanged). Within a tier the **block size** (the padding of a block, the space round its rows and its title, the gap between blocks, the space under the grid on page 1; the type sizes never change) is a scale **u from 0 to 1**: 0 is the **smallest allowed size** (the `tight` values of the tier in `DENSITIES`: for example in the compact tier 6.5 pt of padding, 5 pt between blocks and a block never under 54 pt) and 1 is the **largest** (the tier's own padding plus what its `cap` allows). The rule: **(1)** work out the **smallest number of pages** the days can take at the smallest size (the days in order, as many on a page as fit: P); **(2)** within P pages use the **largest size** (the largest u, found by halving the interval, at which every day still fits in P pages): the same size on every page, a day block always whole; **(3)** **share the days out over exactly P pages** as evenly as the block heights allow (a small dynamic programme that minimises the squared empty part of each page), so the last page is not left with a few days. The days on each page are, for real itineraries: **1 day** 1 page; **3 days** 1; **5 days** 2/3 (days on page 1/2); **10 days** 4/6; **14 days** 6/8 (two pages; the old rule gave three); **19 days** 5/7/7 (three pages; the old rule gave four, 4/6/6/3); and a hand-written **28 days** 6/7/7/8 (four pages). The stored generator writes a week at a time beyond 21 nights, so a 27-night stay is four blocks on one page. Pages are within a few percent of each other in how full they are; the tests sweep 1 to 30 days and check that every page of a trip of several pages is at least 75 percent full and the last at least 80 percent of the fullest. `planItinerary(model)` returns the layout without drawing it (the pages, the blocks, the scale used, the fill, and `sizesAt(u)` for the tests, which count the pages again by themselves), for the tests and for a report.
- **The date labels in the band** (step 8h-4). The words of a turned label ("DAY 1", "31 MAR", "TO 21 MAR") are set **one by one with the same gap between the ink of one word and the ink of the next** (0.36 of the type size, `INK_GAP` in `documentBuilder.js`, `rotatedText`) instead of with the font's space, whose width is fixed (0.2 em), so the gap that is seen depended on the shapes on either side ("2 APR", whose A leans away, looked tight beside "15 MAR"; the gap measured between 0.24 and 0.35 em). The ink of the whole label is centred on the block. In the file the words of a label are separate pieces of text, one above the other along the turned line (a reader that extracts text still sees a space between them). A test measures the gap from the font's real glyph edges for 1 MAY, 28 FEB, 31 MAR, 2 APR, 10 JAN and four more, and for TO 21 MAR: all alike within 0.01 em.
- **Draft rule, refusal, audit, file name, button.** As the cover letter's. The newest version is exported; if it is not approved, every page carries the **banner and the diagonal watermark, drawn over the blocks at a low opacity** (the tinted blocks would hide a watermark drawn behind them), and the file ends "-draft". Text the fonts cannot print is refused with **422 `PDF_UNSUPPORTED_CHARACTERS`** and `section` `itinerary` (the text) or `details` (the traveller's name or an answer in the grid). The audit row is `pdf_export` with `pdfType: "itinerary"`, `pdfBy: "admin"`, `version` and `pdfApproved`, **never any text**; the Activity panel says "downloaded version 2 of the itinerary as a PDF (a draft, not approved)". The **"Download PDF" button** is in the Itinerary panel, with the same states and messages as the cover letter's (off until an itinerary exists, a draft said to be marked, unsaved text said not to be in the file).

**What is next.** The PDF work is done for the team. A PDF for the applicant of the letter or the itinerary, and the entry points, are not part of it (sections 3 and 14). The foundation's pieces: the page setup, fonts, header, footer, page numbers, rules, tint colours and text measuring are in `pdfBuilder.js`; the designed-document page (the draft mark, the compact header, the slim footer, the edge, turned text) is `documentBuilder.js`; the normalisation and the refusal check are in `pdfText.js`; the words, the palette (`BRAND`) and the three page profiles (`PAGE`, `LETTER_PAGE`, `ITINERARY_PAGE`) are in `touristVisaPdf.js`.

## 4. The feature switches

The form is open only when **the server says open** (4.1) **and this build's own hold passes** (4.2). The server decides first and alone: a closed server can never be opened from a client.

### 4.1 Server switch: `TOURIST_VISA_ENABLED` (backend)

- The API is **open only when the variable is exactly the text `true`**. Unset, empty, `false`, `TRUE`, ` true`, `1` and anything else keep it closed. The default is closed, so a deployment that forgot to set it is inert.
- While closed, **every request under `/api/tourist-visa`** (any method, any path, with or without a token) gets the same answer: HTTP 404 with `{"success": false, "code": "FEATURE_CLOSED", "message": "The Tourist Visa application is not open."}`. Nothing the caller sent is echoed back.
- It answers **before** sign-in checks, body parsing, rate limiting, sessions and any database access. The gate (`backend/middleware/touristVisaFeatureGate.js`) is mounted in `backend/index.js` straight after the CORS check, and is repeated at the top of the Tourist Visa router. It reads no header, body or token, touches no database and **writes nothing to the log**. A closed deployment does not even complain about a missing `FIELD_ENCRYPTION_KEY`.
- The CORS check still runs first, so a browser on an allowed origin can read the closed answer, and a request from an origin that is not allowed is rejected exactly as it is on every other route.
- The code `FEATURE_CLOSED` is how clients tell it from the API's own 404 "you have not started an application yet", which has no code.
- The value is read on each request, but the host only changes it when the backend is restarted or redeployed. Treat a change as needing a restart.
- **The one exception: `GET /api/tourist-visa/status`.** It needs no sign-in and returns `200 { "open": true }` only when the variable is exactly `true`, otherwise `200 { "open": false }`: nothing else, no echo of the request, no cookie, no log line and no database access. It is how the client learns whether to show the feature. It sends `Cache-Control: public, max-age=60` and goes through the **general rate limiter** (it is mounted right after it, before sessions and the router). The exception is exactly one route: the gate lets through only a `GET` whose path is exactly `/status`; `POST /status`, `/status/`, `/status/x`, `/statusx`, `/Status` and every other request under the prefix still get the fixed 404 `FEATURE_CLOSED` before sign-in, body parsing and the database, and the router's own gate has no exception at all. Tests prove this in-process and through the real `index.js` for every kind of value (unset, empty, `false`, `TRUE`, `1`, `yes`, ` true`, `0` and so on all give `open: false`).

### 4.2 The client follows the server, and keeps its own hold

- **It asks the server.** `useTouristVisaOpen()` (`client/src/hooks/useTouristVisaOpen.ts`) calls the status route once per page load and shares the answer (an in-memory cache for 30 seconds, one ask under way at a time, a failed ask remembered for 5 seconds, a 5-second timeout, the browser's own cache bypassed). It returns `unknown` until the answer arrives, then `open` or `closed`. **Anything but a 200 whose body has `open` exactly `true` is closed**: a network error, a timeout, a 404 or 500, an HTML page, `{ "open": "yes" }`, an array. A wrong guess can therefore only hide the feature.
- **Open means both.** `useTouristVisaAvailability()` combines the server's answer with this build's own hold (`availabilityOf` in `client/src/utils/touristVisaChecklist.ts`): it is **open** only when the server says open **and** no section is held by an open `tbc` item or a `draft` marker, or the build has `NEXT_PUBLIC_TOURIST_VISA_PREVIEW=true`. The hold and the preview flag are unchanged; the preview flag **cannot open a closed server**. The approved config holds nothing, so today the server's answer alone decides.
- **Three states.** `loading` shows a neutral wait (nothing of the feature, no menu entry, no other Tourist Visa call); `closed` shows the plain page "This application is not available yet" with a link home (for applicants at `/tourist-visa` and every section, for administrators at `/admin/tourist-visa` and the Overview, with a link back to the admin area); `open` shows the feature. A closed deployment shows no dashboard, no error box and no login prompt for the Tourist Visa pages.
- **Where it is used:** the Tourist Visa layout (the provider that calls the API is mounted only when open, so a closed or unknown state makes no other call), the two admin pages (they ask the API only when open) and the admin menu (the entry "Tourist Visa" is there only when open, never while the answer is pending).
- **Step 8i reuses it** (section 2.9): the footer link, the popup's button and the carried `next` all show only when `useTouristVisaAvailability()` is `open`, and nothing while it is `loading`, `closed` or failed.
- Off for the preview flag means unset or any value other than the exact text `true`. It is a `NEXT_PUBLIC_` variable, read **when the client is built**; leave it unset in production.
- Preview mode also shows the TBC and Draft badges (none appear with the approved config). Use it in development and on a private test deployment only.

### 4.3 What an applicant sees

Whichever side says closed (or the answer could not be had), a visitor sees the **same page**: "This application is not available yet. We're not taking Tourist Visa applications at the moment. Please check back soon.", with a link to the home page; no dashboard, no Start button, no error box, and no sign-in prompt. The client knows from the status call, before it loads anything else. If the server is switched off while someone has a section open, their next save is answered with the API's own closed answer (a 404 with `FEATURE_CLOSED`) and the page says "The application isn't open yet"; the next page load shows the page above.

### 4.4 Combinations

| Backend `TOURIST_VISA_ENABLED` | Client build | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| unset or not `true` | anything | **Closed. The API is inert** (only `/status` answers, `open: false`). Applicants see "not available yet"; the admin menu has no entry. **This is the production default.** |
| `true` | no preview, and a `tbc` or `draft` has been added to the config | The server is open but this build's own hold is closed: applicants see the same "not available yet" page. **Avoid in production**: the API is live and a signed-in user could call it directly. (Not the case with config `1.1.0`.) |
| `true` | preview `true` | Open, with TBC and Draft badges where the config has them. **Test builds only.** The preview flag does not open a closed server. |
| `true` | no preview, no `tbc` and no draft left (**config `1.1.0`**) | **Open. This is the public release.** |

**The server's switch is the single switch to operate.** Keep it off until the release and turn it on as the last step (section 9.2); to close it again, unset it and restart the backend. No client rebuild or redeploy is needed in either direction: a client built at any time shows the closed page while the server says closed. (Before the client followed the server, a client whose own gate was open while the server was closed showed a dashboard that could not load; that can no longer happen.)

Important: **a draft keeps this build's own hold closed, exactly like a `tbc` item.** Nothing is ever confirmed silently: a change held back for review keeps the client closed until someone removes its `draft` line (and settles every `tbc`) in `shared/tourist-visa/questions.json`. The release check says the same thing: it exits 1 while anything is blocked by a `tbc`, 2 when nothing is blocked but drafts still need senior review, and **0 only when the client's own hold would pass**. Draft wording under `declarations` holds the form back in the same way. Run the check before every production deploy (section 8) and treat anything other than 0 as "do not release yet". **Today the config carries neither, so the check exits 0 and the hold passes; what keeps production closed is the server switch, which the client follows.**

## 7. Database

The feature adds **one new collection**, `touristvisaapplications` (model `TouristVisaApplication`), with three indexes: one live draft per applicant (unique, partial), `applicant + createdAt`, and `status + createdAt`. It does not read or change any existing collection except `users` (to identify the signed-in user, as the rest of the site does).

**Step 8h-1 (PDFs): what is new in the database.** Nothing is stored: a PDF is made in memory and sent. The audit collection gains one action name, `pdf_export`, and two fields, `pdfType` and `pdfBy` (section 2.8); `adminId` holds the applicant's own user id on an applicant's download. Step 8h-2 adds a third field, `pdfApproved` (a yes or no; with the existing `version` it names the printed version of a cover letter). No collection, index or existing row changes, and no stored letter or application is touched. Step 8h-3 adds no field: an itinerary row uses the same `pdfType` (`itinerary`), `version` and `pdfApproved`, and no stored itinerary is touched.

**The itinerary: what is new in the database.** One new collection, **`touristvisaitineraries`** (section 2.7): one row per version of an itinerary, the text encrypted, append only apart from the one-time approval, with the same fields as the cover letter's. The audit collection gains four action names (`itinerary_generate`, `itinerary_view`, `itinerary_edit`, `itinerary_approve`) and, from the polish step, the two fields `version` and `source` (the itinerary's rows use them; older itinerary rows with `letterVersion` and `letterSource` stay valid and are read as before; the cover letter's rows are unchanged). The daily and per-administrator AI limits are now counted over both `touristvisacoverletters` and `touristvisaitineraries`. No existing application is rewritten and no config changes. **On a deployment where automatic index building is switched off, create the indexes once by hand** (section 2.7).

**Step 8g: what is new in the database.** One new collection, **`touristvisacoverletters`** (section 2.6): one row per version of a cover letter, the text encrypted, append only apart from the one-time approval. The audit collection gains four action names (`cover_letter_generate`, `cover_letter_view`, `cover_letter_edit`, `cover_letter_approve`) and three fields (`letterVersion`, `letterSource`, `model`). No existing application is rewritten and no config changes. **On a deployment where automatic index building is switched off, create the indexes once by hand** (section 2.6).

**Step 8f: what is new in the database.** Two new collections, **`touristvisachanges`** (the correction records, index `applicationId + at`) and **`touristvisanotes`** (notes and the call log, index `applicationId + at`), both append only by the application's own rules (section 2.4). Each application gains two optional fields: `revision` (a number; a missing one counts as 0, so nothing needs migrating) and `adminFlags`. The audit collection gains seven action names and the `fromFlags` and `toFlags` fields. **On a deployment where automatic index building is switched off, create them once by hand**: `db.touristvisachanges.createIndex({ applicationId: 1, at: -1 })` and `db.touristvisanotes.createIndex({ applicationId: 1, at: -1 })`. **Correcting an application needs a MongoDB that supports transactions (a replica set).** Atlas does; a standalone local MongoDB cannot (the route answers 503 `TRANSACTIONS_UNAVAILABLE` and writes nothing). No existing application is rewritten by this step.

**Step 8e: what is new in the database.** There is one new collection, **`touristvisaaudit`** (section 2.3): append only by the application's own rules, with indexes on `applicationId + at` and `adminId + at`. Each application gains an optional `summary` (`{ name, email, phone }`, plain text, written at submit and filled in once for older submitted applications) and one new index, `status + submittedAt`, for the list. **On a deployment where automatic index building is switched off, create them once by hand**: `db.touristvisaapplications.createIndex({ status: 1, submittedAt: -1 })`, `db.touristvisaaudit.createIndex({ applicationId: 1, at: -1 })` and `db.touristvisaaudit.createIndex({ adminId: 1, at: -1 })`. No existing application is rewritten except by the one-time summary fill. **Applications submitted before 8e have no `summary` until an admin lists or opens them.**

Each application records the `configVersion` its answers were collected with. When the config changes, saved answers are never rewritten: the server works out the status of each section when it reads the application, and a section whose answers no longer fit shows as **Needs review**.

**Step 8d: what is new in the database.** Each application gains two optional fields, `reference` and `configSha256` (set only at submit), and `declarations.submit`. There is one new collection, `touristvisaconfigsnapshots` (section 2.2), and one new index: a **unique, partial index on `reference`** (only documents that have a text reference are in it). Mongoose builds it when the server starts; **on a deployment where automatic index building is switched off, create it once by hand**: `db.touristvisaapplications.createIndex({ reference: 1 }, { unique: true, partialFilterExpression: { reference: { $type: "string" } } })`. ## 9. Deploying

### 9.1 Deploy with the switch OFF (the production default)

From the repository root, check the branch first. The release check is expected to exit **0** (nothing is held), so the client gate is open in this build; **the deployment stays inert only because the backend switch is off.**

```
git fetch && git checkout feature/tourist-visa-form && git pull
npm run check:shared
npm run test:shared
(cd backend && npm ci && npm test)            # keep 1 GB free on the temp drive
(cd client  && npm ci && npm run test:unit && npx tsc --noEmit && npx next build)
npm run check:tourist-visa-release            # expect exit 0
```

Then:

1. Merge through your normal pull request. Check `backend/package-lock.json` (section 6).
2. **Backend environment: do not set `TOURIST_VISA_ENABLED`** (or leave it empty). Confirm `JWT_SECRET` is set. You may already generate and store `FIELD_ENCRYPTION_KEY` (section 5) so that opening it later is a single change; it is not used while the switch is off.
3. Deploy the backend.
4. **Client build: leave `NEXT_PUBLIC_TOURIST_VISA_PREVIEW` unset**, then build and deploy.
5. Check it is inert:
   - `curl -i https://<your-api>/api/tourist-visa/applications/me` returns **404** with `"code":"FEATURE_CLOSED"`, with or without a token, and `POST` and `PUT` to the same prefix answer the same.
   - Other routes still work, for example `GET /api/approved-visas` returns 200.
   - The backend log shows nothing about `/api/tourist-visa` requests and no `FIELD_ENCRYPTION_KEY` error.
   - `curl -i https://<your-api>/api/tourist-visa/status` returns **200** `{"open":false}` (the one route that answers while closed).
   - `/tourist-visa` shows "This application is not available yet" with a link home (no dashboard, signed in or not), and the admin menu has no "Tourist Visa" entry.
   - Nothing links to the form.

### 9.2 Turning it ON later

1. The design is approved (section 10) and `npm run check:tourist-visa-release` must exit **0**. If a later change added a `draft` or a `tbc`, exit 2 or 1 keeps the client closed and you should not release until it is removed.
2. Add the entry-point links (Footer, Navbar, Services) as a separate change.
3. Generate `FIELD_ENCRYPTION_KEY` if you have not (section 5), store it in the secret store and back it up. **Do not skip the backup.**
4. In this order:
   1. Deploy the backend with the new config and the key set, **still without `TOURIST_VISA_ENABLED`**. The API is still closed.
   2. Build and deploy the client **without** `NEXT_PUBLIC_TOURIST_VISA_PREVIEW`. It follows the server: while the server answers "closed", applicants see "not available yet" and the admin menu has no entry, so nothing is exposed. (The client can be deployed before or after the backend; it only ever shows what the server says.)
   3. **Set `TOURIST_VISA_ENABLED=true` on the backend and restart or redeploy it. This is the moment the form opens**: the next page load of every client sees `open: true` (a client keeps an answer for at most 30 seconds, and the status response may be cached for up to 60 seconds by a proxy).
5. Smoke test with a throwaway account: start an application, save a section, and confirm in the database that the passport and ID numbers are stored as `enc:v1:...`. Look at the backend log for errors.
6. **To close it again, instantly and for everyone:** unset `TOURIST_VISA_ENABLED` and restart or redeploy the backend. No client rebuild is needed: the next page load shows the closed page and the menu entry is gone. Saved data stays in the database.

**Test deployments** (private, never public): backend `TOURIST_VISA_ENABLED=true` and a client build with `NEXT_PUBLIC_TOURIST_VISA_PREVIEW=true`.

**Your own machine:** add the line `TOURIST_VISA_ENABLED=true` to `backend/.env`, and restart the backend. Without it your local backend answers "closed" to every Tourist Visa request.

### 10.9 Other choices we made (not flagged `tbc`)

- **Limits:** 200 characters for short text, 5,000 for long text, 20 entries per group (the spec's proposals, marked as unconfirmed in the config). The long questions added in step 7c (5.1d and 5.1l) are limited to 2,000 characters; lowering the older ones would make saved answers invalid, so they were left alone.
- **Sensitive fields:** 36 answers are encrypted: the national identity card number (2.19), the passport number (3.1), the UK driving licence number (2.30a), the medical treatment text (2.31b), the UK visa reference (7.2c), the leave-to-remain reference (7.3f) the breach details (7.6d), the ten Yes/No answers and ten details boxes of Part 9 (9.1 to 9.10, 9.1a to 9.10a), and, from step 8b-2, the penalties checklist and its six boxes (10.1, 10.1a to 10.1f), the National Insurance Yes/No (10.5) and the number (10.5a). Other personal and financial answers (family names, dates of birth, employer, income, savings) are stored as typed. Decide whether any of those should be encrypted too, but read the limitation below first.
- **Limitation: an existing question cannot simply be made encrypted.** Marking a question `sensitive` only affects answers saved from then on. If any application already holds that answer as plain text, reading it fails ("Your saved answers could not be read", HTTP 500), because the server expects ciphertext for a sensitive field. Making an existing question encrypted therefore needs a one-off migration that encrypts the stored values first (each value is bound to its application and field, so it must be done by the server's own cipher). For this reason steps 7b, 7d, 8b-1 and 8b-2 encrypt **only new fields** (2.30a, 2.31b, 7.2c, 7.3f, 7.6d, the Part 9 answers and the Part 10 answers listed in section 10.6) and change no existing question. **This is a known limitation, and the immigration-problem text (7.7d), the payer details and the other long free-text answers stay unencrypted for the same reason.** Before any real applicant data exists the change is harmless; after that it needs the migration.
- **Sanity rules the spec does not state:** dates of birth and past events cannot be in the future; amounts are in GBP, never negative and capped (5.7, 5.1e, 5.1j, 6.1a, 6.3, 6.4, 6.5f and 6.8e at 1,000,000; 6.2 at 100,000,000; the medical amount owing 2.31e at 100,000); the "past 10 years" limit applies to 7.2a, 7.3b, 7.5b and 8.2d (the leave date), not to 7.6c or 7.7c ("ever"), and trips abroad in 8.2 may overlap.
- **2.1 to 2.35:** email 2.4 is pre-filled from the account email and can be edited; 2.21 and 2.22 are required whenever 2.18 is Yes; 2.27 is two boxes (years, months, months at most 11); an address needs line 1, town and country.
- **Hidden sections and old answers:** a section that does not apply (4A) cannot be saved, and the server removes its answers when the gate closes.

### 10.10 How to change a question later

The senior sends a change as a **question number or a part** ("question 2.8: add Other to the list"). To make it:

1. Edit **only `shared/tourist-visa/questions.json`**: a label, help text, option list, rule, follow-up or a new question. **Never change or reuse an `id`**: stored answers sit under it. To retire a question, hide it (a condition that is never met) or remove it, knowing its stored answers stay in the database untouched and are no longer shown (section 7).
2. Run `npm run sync:shared` (copies to `client/src/shared` and `backend/shared`), then `npm run check:shared`.
3. **Bump `version`** (it is recorded on every application as `configVersion`): `1.0.1` for wording or an extra option, `1.1.0` for a new or removed question or a new rule, `2.0.0` for a redesign. Nothing is migrated whatever the number: it tells people which questions an application was collected with.
4. Run `npm run test:shared`, the backend tests and the client tests, and update the tests that name the changed values. The test "wording hashes: every Part 9 tick and the final declaration have exactly the hash they had..." pins the hash of the four ticks and of the final declaration: it fails if a tick or the declaration wording changes, so such a change is always deliberate (and applicants who ticked the old words will tick again).
5. `npm run check:tourist-visa-release` must exit 0. **To hold a change back for review before release**, add a `draft` line with a short note (or a `tbc` line for a real unknown) to that question: the check then exits 2 (or 1), the client gate closes, and preview mode shows the badge, exactly as before. Remove the line when the change is approved.
6. Deploy the backend and the client from the same commit (each carries its own copy of the config). **The release switches do not change**: `TOURIST_VISA_ENABLED` decides whether the form is open, and a config change touches neither it nor the client's own hold (unless it adds a `draft` or `tbc`).

**What happens to applications already in the system:**

- **Not yet submitted (drafts).** Every saved answer is kept. The server works out each section's status against the new config each time it reads the application: a saved section whose answers no longer pass shows **Needs review**; a new question shows as needing an answer; an answer to a question that no longer exists stays stored, untouched and hidden; an answer that is hidden only because its Yes/No was never answered is kept (section 2). A tick whose wording changed is shown unticked until it is given again. Submitting re-checks everything against the new config.
- **Already submitted.** Never rewritten and never re-validated. Each is shown, to the applicant (review and read-only page), in the admin Overview and in the admin Edit forms, with **the config snapshot it was submitted under** (`configSha256`, section 2.2), so a later change to a label or an option never changes what was submitted. An admin's correction is validated against that same snapshot (section 2.4). Declarations already given keep their record and stay valid.
- A test (`touristVisaConfigApproved.test.js`) shows both cases with the move from `0.12.0-draft` to `1.0.x`.

## 11. Not part of this feature, found while reviewing

These are existing problems in the rest of the application. They were found while reading the code to build this feature, and **none of them was changed**. They are listed so they are not lost. The new Tourist Visa routes do not have these problems.

1. **Unauthenticated routes.** These accept requests without a signed-in user:
   - `/api/submit-assessment`, `/api/submit-assessment-local`, `/api/test-pdf-upload`, `/api/merge-pdfs`, `/api/compress-pdfs`
   - `GET /api/form-submissions/customer-submission/:email`
   - `POST /api/form-submissions/:id/customer-comment`
   - `GET /api/form-submissions/file/:submissionId/:documentId`
2. **Public Cloudinary URLs.** Uploaded documents are stored as public `secure_url` links, with a 10 MB limit and DOC, DOCX and GIF allowed. Anyone who has a link can open the file. The Tourist Visa spec asks for private files with expiring links; that will need `type: "authenticated"` uploads and signed URLs, not the existing middleware.
3. **Global input sanitizer.** `backend/middleware/sanitize.js` strips `<` and `>` from every string on every route except the new Tourist Visa path, which can silently alter user text.
4. **Error logging.** `backend/middleware/errorHandler.js` logs the whole error object for unexpected errors. Database cast errors can include the submitted value, so personal data can end up in logs. The Tourist Visa routes use their own log-safe handler; the rest of the app does not.
5. **Default session secret (fixed in step 8j-2b).** `backend/index.js` used to fall back to the string `"your-secret-key"` when `SESSION_SECRET` was not set. The session middleware was removed with the Google and GitHub sign-in, so the setting is no longer read.
6. **A database connection string with credentials in the git history of main.** Two old commits (`2117279` and `6e1ad3a`) added a `mongodb+srv://user:password@...` string to `backend/config/db.js`. ### 14.2 Cover letter (step 8g): the original plan, built differently

**Built as section 2.6.** The senior asked for an AI, so the letter is written by an AI from an allowlisted set of facts with a deterministic template as the fallback (the plan below said "No AI"); it is admin only, on demand, with versions, an approval and a stale flag. The plan below is kept for what is still true of it (the template, the placeholders for a missing answer, versions, the hand edit with a notice, encryption, the PDF in step 8h).

A cover letter is generated from the applicant's answers when the application is submitted.

- **Deterministic template.** No AI, no invented facts. Every sentence is built only from an answer; text that depends on an answer shows only when that answer exists. A missing answer leaves a visible placeholder (for example "[not provided: planned arrival date]"), never a guess. The same answers always give the same letter.
- **Approval.** The senior approves the template wording before it is built. Like the questions, the template carries a draft marker with a version and is listed by the release check until it is approved. **Decided:** Parts 9 and 10A (the security and penalty answers) never feed the cover letter, and neither do the rest of 10.1 to 10.5 (the extra-information boxes, the language and the National Insurance number). The senior still decides which of the other answers do.
- **Stored encrypted** with the same field cipher, bound to the application. Each generation or edit is a new version; earlier versions stay in the history.
- **Regenerated** when the admin corrects answers (8f). **A correction that touches an answer the letter uses must create a new letter version**: the change records (section 2.4) list the answers each correction touched. **Decided:** a letter the admin has edited by hand is kept in the history, a new generated version is added, and the admin sees a notice.
- **Editable by the admin**, with a required reason; every edit is logged in the audit trail (admin, time, old and new text encrypted), and two admins cannot overwrite each other (revision check).
- **Downloadable as a PDF** by admins only, and each download is logged. This adds the first PDF output; step 8h reuses it.
- Proposed: admin-only in the first version (the applicant does not see the letter).
- Tests: the same answers give the same text, a missing answer gives a placeholder and nothing is invented, regeneration after a correction, ciphertext in the database, edits logged, and the PDF.

Admins will be able to read every applicant's decrypted answers, including passport numbers and the security-history answers, and to change them. It is recommended to add **multi-factor authentication for admin accounts** before the admin Overview (built in step 8e, section 2.3) is used with real applicants. It is a change to the existing login, outside the Tourist Visa code, so it is recorded here rather than planned as a step; the view log and the audit trail (steps 8e and 8f) show who looked at or changed what, but do not stop a stolen password.

### 14.5 For the security review (step 8j)

Things to look at, collected as the steps were built:

- **The reference** (8d): the format `TV-` + 8 characters of a 31-character alphabet (about 850 billion), drawn with `crypto.randomInt`, unique-indexed, shown to the applicant and meant to be quoted. It must never be used to look an application up or to authorise anything. It can be read out loud, so a person may guess a mistyped one: nothing should confirm that a reference exists to anyone but its owner or an admin.
- **The config snapshot** (8d): stored as text in its own collection, keyed by its SHA-256, written once and never changed or deleted; it holds the form's questions and wording only. Check that nothing can write to or read from that collection except the submit and the review code, and that an admin or PDF path never falls back to the live config for a submitted application.
- **What a submit logs** (8d): at most one line with the application's id, in development only; the refusal and the 500 carry ids and counts, never an answer. Re-check this after every change to the submit.
- **Done in 8j-3 (B9): a limit on submit** (8d): 15 an hour for each signed-in user, with its own budget (section 14.6). It was: submit and review sit behind sign-in and the general API limiter only.

### 14.6 Step 8j-3: the findings fixed inside the Tourist Visa feature (built, manually tested and approved)

Five findings of the 8j-1 review that sit inside the Tourist Visa feature were fixed. Nothing outside the feature was changed except two tiny shared helpers (the list of allowed origins, now one file that CORS and the new guard both read; and the key reader, which now takes an environment). The Google and GitHub sign-in code was not touched here (it was removed separately, in 8j-2b).

**A11, the feature part: safe start-up.** `backend/config/touristVisaStartup.js`, called first thing by `index.js`. While `TOURIST_VISA_ENABLED` is exactly `true` the server **exits with code 1** and a message that names the variables (never a value) unless: `FIELD_ENCRYPTION_KEY` is set and is a valid 32-byte key; `JWT_SECRET` is set; and, when `AI_ENABLED` is `true`, `AI_PROVIDER` is `groq` and `GROQ_API_KEY` is set. While the switch is off nothing is checked and nothing is logged (a closed deployment behaves exactly as before). The AI data-terms guard (`config/ai.js`) used to count as production only when `NODE_ENV` was exactly `production`; it now counts as production **unless `NODE_ENV` is exactly `development` or `test`**. With AI on in production and `AI_DATA_TERMS_CONFIRMED` not `true` the behaviour is as it was and not a refusal: the **AI stays off** for every letter and itinerary (the template is used, the code `AI_TERMS_NOT_CONFIRMED`); the only addition is a clear line in the log at start-up that names the variable. **Not done (older code, open for the senior):** the checks for `SESSION_SECRET`, `MONGODB_URI`, `FRONTEND_URL`, the Cloudinary three and the refusal of an unknown `NODE_ENV`.

**A12: stale approvals.** The PDF export now uses the same check as the admin panels (the keyed hash of the facts the document was written from, compared with the hash of the answers now), for an application in any status (the panels' own check refuses a completed one, so it was separated into `isStale` on both services). An approved letter or itinerary whose answers changed afterwards is printed as a draft: the diagonal watermark, a banner that reads "DRAFT - ANSWERS CHANGED SINCE APPROVAL" on every page, and "-draft" in the file name. The audit row records `pdfApproved: false` and the new `pdfStale: true` (written only when true; a current document's row is unchanged). The Activity panel says "a draft: the answers had changed since it was approved"; the panel under the document shows the same in the note beside the Download PDF button. If the check itself cannot be worked out, the document is treated as stale (the safe side). A version that was never approved keeps the usual "NOT APPROVED" banner.

**B3: forged cross-site requests.** One middleware, `middleware/touristVisaOriginGuard.js`, on every **state-changing** request (POST, PUT, PATCH, DELETE) under `/api/tourist-visa`. The rule: an `Origin` header, if present, must be exactly one of the site's allowed origins (`config/allowedOrigins.js`, the list CORS uses), otherwise **403 `ORIGIN_NOT_ALLOWED`**; with no `Origin`, the request must carry an `Authorization: Bearer` header, and a request that has a cookie but no Authorization header is a 403 (a request with neither is passed on and gets the usual 401). Why this rule: the client sends the token in the Authorization header on every call and never relies on the cookie, and browsers add `Origin` to every such request, so the client needed no change; a hostile page cannot add an Authorization header without the server's permission. It is mounted in `index.js` before CORS (only while the feature is open, so the closed behaviour is unchanged) and again in the router. Reads are not touched. Older routes are not changed.

**B6: no-store.** One middleware, `middleware/touristVisaNoStore.js`: `Cache-Control: no-store` on every answer under `/api/tourist-visa` (errors, refusals and rate-limit answers included), set in `index.js` and on both routers. The public status answer keeps its short public caching. **Not done:** `/api/login`, `/api/signup` and `/api/auth` (the review also listed them; they are older code).

**B9: rate limits.** `middleware/touristVisaLimits.js`, four limiters with a budget each, counted per signed-in user (per address if there is no user), a 429 with a fixed sentence and a code, separate from the AI, PDF and upload limiters: **submit** 15 an hour (`TOO_MANY_SUBMISSIONS`); **edit form and corrections/previews** 60 in 10 minutes (`TOO_MANY_EDITS`); **notes, call log and flags** 60 in 10 minutes (`TOO_MANY_NOTES`); **admin list and search** 120 in 10 minutes (`TOO_MANY_LIST_REQUESTS`). The upload route already had its own limit (40 an hour for each user) and is unchanged. The real call rate: the form saves a section when the person presses "Save and continue" (there is no timer or auto-save), so an applicant makes a few dozen calls in a long session; the tests run 40 saves, 30 reads and 4 submits without a 429. The client shows the sentence for each code (and a general one for any other 429). The general limiter (`/api`, 100 requests in 15 minutes for each address) is still the tighter limit for a busy office; that is finding A9 and was not changed.

**Variables a local setup needs for the safe start-up** (names only): `TOURIST_VISA_ENABLED=true`, `FIELD_ENCRYPTION_KEY` (a valid key), `JWT_SECRET`; with the AI on, also `AI_ENABLED=true`, `AI_PROVIDER=groq` and `GROQ_API_KEY`; and `NODE_ENV=development` (otherwise the AI counts as production and stays off unless `AI_DATA_TERMS_CONFIRMED=true`). `MONGODB_URI` and `PORT` are needed by the site as before.

**Tests.** The new backend files are listed in section 8. Counts at this step: shared 330, backend 846 (58 new), client 398 (9 new). After main was merged into `fix/critical-auth-hardening` (which also removes the Google and GitHub sign-in): shared 330, backend 903, client 397.
